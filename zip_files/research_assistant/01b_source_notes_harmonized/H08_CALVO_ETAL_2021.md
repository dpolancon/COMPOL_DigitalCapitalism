# Source Note — CALVO & VENTURA 2021 PARTISANSHIP RISK BRAZIL

## Basic information

Author: Ernesto Calvo and Tiago Ventura
Year: 2021
Title: Will I Get COVID-19? Partisanship, Social Media Frames, and Perceptions of Health Risk in Brazil
Journal / book / source: Latin American Politics and Society
PDF filename: PDF008
Source ID: PDF008

## What this source is about

This study examines how political partisanship in Brazil (specifically support for or opposition to Jair Bolsonaro) influenced how people perceived the risks of COVID-19. It tests whether isolated social media messages (frames) can change people's minds compared to major national events like a presidential TV address.

## Method or approach

Mixed-methods:
1. **Natural Experiment (Difference-in-Differences):** Measuring the impact of President Bolsonaro’s March 2024 TV speech by comparing survey responses from just before and just after the broadcast.
2. **Survey Experiment:** A preregistered experiment where 2,400 respondents were shown edited tweets from political leaders (Bolsonaro vs. Haddad) to see if the "frame" (positive vs. negative) changed their risk perception.

## Case, country, platform, and period

Country / region: Brazil
Election or political context: Early COVID-19 pandemic (March–May 2020) and the rise of populist leadership.
Platform: Twitter (experimental context) and National Television (natural experiment).
Time period: March to May 2020.
Type of material studied: Online survey data (Netquest panel) and elite political messaging.

## Main relevance for Diego’s paper

- **LatAm Context (G4):** Provides a key comparison between Chile and Brazil. While Chile's digital politics is often driven by structural inequality (Luna et al.), this paper shows how national politics in Brazil is driven by **negative partisanship** (anti-Petismo).
- **Platformization (G3):** It acts as a "cautionary tale" for the power of digital frames. It proves that in saturated environments, isolated social media posts are often too weak to break through pre-existing partisan filters.
- **Democratic Risk (G5):** Highlights the risk of populist leaders using their platforms to transform scientific facts (like a virus) into polarized "wedge issues."

## Useful claims

- **Partisan Override:** Partisanship (especially negative partisanship) successfully overrides objective facts when voters assess personal risks.
- **Limits of Digital Framing:** Isolated social media frames had "modest to null" effects on voters' beliefs; major "bully pulpit" events (like TV addresses) were far more powerful.
- **Backlash Effect:** When leaders use negative/dismissive messages during a crisis, it can backfire—even among their own supporters—by increasing their sense of job risk.
- **Positive Sharing Bias:** Contrary to the "toxic content" myth, users across all parties were significantly more likely to "like" and "retweet" positive, cooperative messages than negative, polarizing ones.
