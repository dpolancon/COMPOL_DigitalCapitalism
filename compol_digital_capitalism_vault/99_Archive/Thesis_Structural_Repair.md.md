## II. PROPOSED STRUCTURAL SPINE (3 ACTS)

### **ACT 1: The Theoretical-Empirical Problem in the Literature**

_Positions your work as a response to specific gaps_

**Starting Point:** Literature identifies these central debates but leaves them unresolved:

#### A. **Online Microtargeting: How "Micro" Is It Really?**

- International literature: defines microtargeting as "small niches, personalized messages" (Borgesius et al., 2018; López Ortega, 2021)
- **Empirical gap**: few studies open the "black box" of actual targeting vs. rhetoric
- **Your contribution**: analysis of Facebook metadata (age, region, gender) + thematic content
- **Question you resolve**: How is microtargeting actually distributed? Is there real segmentation or disguised broadcasting?

#### B. **Context Matters: Transferability of North American Models to Chile**

- Literature: majority of studies on Obama, Trump, USA (Bimber, 2014; Hanson et al., 2010)
- Chilean literature: disconnected from online microtargeting (Tejada 2016 is the exception)
- **Gap**: How do these practices operate in contexts of **extreme polarization** + **institutional legitimacy crisis** + **political reorganization**?
- **Your contribution**: 2021 case = moment of political reconfiguration (anti-establishment candidates, Boric, Parisi, Kast)
- **Question you resolve**: Does microtargeting amplify radical messages? Does it fragment or polarize?

#### C. **Ethics and Effectiveness: Separating Fears from Facts**

- Literature: Kreiss (2017) vs. Gorton (2016) – is microtargeting _inherently_ manipulative or merely _potentially_ problematic?
- Baldwin-Philippi (2017, 2018): insistence on "empirical grounding" of dystopian fears
- **Gap**: comparative analyses of **thematic content + targeting** in non-USA contexts
- **Your contribution**: What messages were sent? To whom? Is there evidence of "contradictory" or "extremely manipulative" messaging?

### **ACT 2: Political Mobility in 2021 = Context That Explains Strategy**

_Connects Background with observed strategies_

The Chilean context in 2021 is not accidental but _constitutive_ of the observed digital strategies:

#### A. **The Legitimacy Crisis of Traditional Coalitions**

- Transition 1990-2019: two stable blocs (Concertación, Alianza)
- 2019-2021: **bloc dislocation**: candidates from the semi-periphery (Boric, Kast, Parisi)
- **Empirical implication**: if parties/candidates lack consolidated mass media presence, digital targeting is _necessary_ to reach a distrustful electorate

**Finding that confirms this:**

- Both finalists (Boric, Kast) build "robust digital teams" (your Background)
- Parisi: 100% digital campaign from USA = extreme of this strategy
- Marco Enríquez-Ominami: managed coherent political communication on networks (as he historically has)

#### B. **Pandemic + Social Uprising = Acceleration of Campaign Transfer to Digital**

- Pre-COVID: gradual adaptation of social networks to campaigns
- 2021: **physical impossibility of in-person campaigning** + **extreme polarization** = forces digital to center stage

**Finding that confirms this:**

- Volume of ads (1,924 total, 535 Boric_2V, 450 Sichel, 321 Provoste, 167 Kast_2V)
- Temporal distribution: concentration in weeks of political intensity

#### C. **"New Politics" Requires "New Tools"**

- Right: candidates did not come from ruling coalition
- Left: candidates from diverse origins (Communist Party, Convergencia Social, independents)
- Both avoid (or cannot) use traditional mass media channels
- **Solution**: digital segmentation + granular targeting

### **ACT 3: What Your Empirical Analysis Shows About These Debates**

_Integrates findings methodologically with literature questions_

#### A. **On the Reality of "Microtargeting"**

**Literature Question:** How granular is reported microtargeting?

**Finding (Demographic Analysis):**

- Segmentation by age, region, gender = **real segmentation exists**
- But far from being "extreme niche-ism"
- Many ads maintain **broad targeting** (e.g., 18-65 years, multi-regional)
- → Suggests mixed strategy: some ultra-segmented ads, others broadcast-style

**Theoretical Implication:**

- Confirms Baldwin-Philippi (2018): microtargeting in real contexts is **pragmatic, not ideological**
- Contradicts apocalyptic visions: targeting is **limited** by budget + platform constraints
- Suggests: the power of microtargeting lies in **potential capacity** more than actually-exercised practice

#### B. **On Thematic Strategies by Political Sector**

**Literature Question:** How do ideological differences operate in digital content?

- Left: education, health, pensions, "sustainability," care (Boric)
- Right: liberty, family, security, "hope," Araucanía (Sichel, Kast)

**Your Finding (Topic Modeling):**

- Left: **high thematic coherence** (social rights as guiding axis)
- Right: **lower coherence** (possible explanation: greater ideological distance between Sichel ± neoliberal/centrist vs. Kast ± far-right)

**Theoretical Implication:**

- Political personalization (Metz et al., 2019; Nusselder, 2013) REMAINS central: candidates as ideological vehicles
- But Chilean ideological context generates **asymmetric fragmentation**: right more fragmented than left
- Microtargeting amplifies **pre-existing differences**, it does not create them

#### C. **On Ethics and Manipulation: Separating Rhetoric from Reality**

**Literature Question:** Evidence of extreme manipulation via microtargeting?

**Your Finding:**

- No candidate sends "contradictory messages" designed to deceive different groups about their fundamental position
- Boric: consistently pro-reform, pro-social rights (same message for different ages/regions)
- Kast: consistently pro-security, pro-family (stable messaging)
- **Variation:** thematic emphasis by region (e.g., Araucanía → security/violence) but not contradiction

**Theoretical Implication:**

- Supports Kreiss (2017) thesis: fears about microtargeting reflect **pre-existing anxieties** about democracy
- Chilean microtargeting 2021 operates in logic of **targeted emphasis**, not **fundamental manipulation**
- Critical distinction: matching messages to audiences vs. creating falsehoods for specific groups
