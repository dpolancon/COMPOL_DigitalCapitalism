### Bibliographic Identity & Core Analysis
*   **Source:** Larsson, A. O., Tønnesen, H., Magin, M., & Skogerbø, E. (2024). A framework for comparing political actors' campaign strategies across social media platforms. *New Media & Society*, *27*(7), 3807–3828.
*   **What it studies:** The strategic use of "calls to action" (imperative statements) by political actors to prompt citizen engagement (information, mobilization, interaction) across different social media platforms.
*   **Method:** Standardized manual content analysis. (Note: This is a qualitative/quantitative hybrid manual method, not computational).
*   **Corpus:** Campaign posts by Norwegian political actors on Facebook, Instagram, and Twitter during the 2021 parliamentary election campaign.
*   **Core Claims:**
    1.  Calls to action are shaped by three factors: audiences, affordances, and genres.
    2.  Platform characteristics significantly affect which types of calls to action are used (e.g., linking features enable "calls to inform").
    3.  Mobilization calls (specifically calls to vote) are most prevalent, especially near election day.
*   **Relevance to Diego's Paper:** (Assuming Diego's paper focuses on digital political campaigns) This serves as a strong **theoretical and empirical precedent** for comparing platforms manually. It offers a robust framework for understanding *why* strategies differ by platform, which can complement or contrast with computational findings in Diego's work.
*   **Cluster Tags:** `#PlatformComparison`, `#CallsToAction`, `#CampaignStrategy`, `#ManualContentAnalysis`, `#Norway`, `#SocialMediaPolitics`.
*   **Possible Citation Uses:**
    *   As a primary source for the "audiences, affordances, genres" framework.
    *   As a benchmark for manual content analysis in multi-platform studies.
    *   To support the argument that election campaigns are "party-centered" (West-European style) vs. "candidate-centered" (US style).
*   **Limits:** Limited to Norway (West-European context); does not cover "platformization" as a critical data-discipline concept; relies on manual coding which may be less scalable than computational methods.
*   **Open Questions:** How do the findings translate to non-Western contexts? How do algorithmic changes impact the "affordances" identified?

### Concept Extraction: Digitalization & Platform Framing
*   **Digitalization/Platformization:** The source does not explicitly use the terms "digitalization" or "platformization" as critical theoretical concepts (e.g., in the vein of van Dijck). Instead, it frames the digital environment through **"affordances"** (functionalities/features) and **"genres"** (communicative styles). It views social media as a tool for "citizen engagement" and "voter mobilization."
*   **Actors:** Political actors (parties), campaign professionals, citizens/audiences.
*   **Platform Framing (Channel vs. Infrastructure):** **Channel/Occasion.** The text treats platforms as distinct environments with specific "affordances" (linking, interaction) that constrain or enable specific campaign functions. It does not treat them as "infrastructure" in a datafied sense (i.e., it does not discuss data extraction, surveillance, or algorithmic governance deeply).
*   **Geographic Scope:** **Local/National** (Norway, specifically 2021 parliamentary election).
*   **Mechanisms (Platforms to Strategy):**
    *   **Affordances:** Specific features (e.g., "linking features" on Facebook) dictate strategy (e.g., "read more" calls to inform).
    *   **Audiences:** Demographics and attentiveness shape the type of action requested.
    *   **Campaign Timing:** Intensity of calls to action increases as election day approaches.
