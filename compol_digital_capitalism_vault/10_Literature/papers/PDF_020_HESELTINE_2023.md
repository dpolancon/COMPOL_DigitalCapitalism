### 1. Bibliographic Identity & Core Content
*   **Bibliographic Identity:** Heseltine, Michael. (2023). *Online Media Engagement in the United States Congress*. The International Journal of Press/Politics, 30(1), 299–325.
*   **What the source studies:** The evolution of media engagement strategies by US Members of Congress (specifically on Twitter and Facebook) and the relationship between elite engagement, ideology, and polarization.
*   **Method and Corpus:** Quantitative analysis of a massive dataset consisting of 6.3 million tweets and 2.3 million Facebook posts sent by Members of Congress between 2011 and 2022 (covering roughly Congresses 112–115+).
*   **Core Claims:**
    1.  There is a clear trend of **asymmetric polarization** in media engagement.
    2.  **Republicans** increasingly engage with right-wing and alternative media (e.g., Fox News, Breitbart), while Democrats remain more focused on mainstream or left-leaning outlets.
    3.  This trend is driven by **Member ideology and behavior**, not by changes in the composition of Congress (e.g., freshman/senior cohorts show similar trends).
    4.  Messages linking to extreme media receive higher public engagement, disproportionately benefiting Republican Members.

It provides empirical evidence of how elite social media usage correlates with polarization.
