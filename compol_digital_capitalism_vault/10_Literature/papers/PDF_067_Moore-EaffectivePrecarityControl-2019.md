### 1. Bibliographic Identity & Source Overview
*   **Bibliographic Identity:** Moore, P. V. (2019). E(a)ffective Precarity, Control and Resistance in the Digitalised Workplace. In Chandler, D. and Fuchs, C. (eds.). *Digital Objects, Digital Subjects: Interdisciplinary Perspectives on Capitalism, Labour and Politics in the Age of Big Data*. London: University of Westminster Press. Pp. 125–144.
*   **What the source studies:** The impact of digitalization on the modern workplace, specifically focusing on the "gig economy," affective labor (emotional/mood labor), and the mechanisms of control used by management.
*   **Method:** Theoretical analysis drawing on autonomist Marxist thought (Negri, Adorno, Federici) and cybernetics (Shannon). It references empirical findings (likely interviews/cases) regarding workers' resistance and the use of self-tracking/surveillance devices, though specific methodology details are not fully elaborated in this chapter excerpt.
*   **Corpus:** UK employment statistics (2016/2017), examples of gig platforms (Upwork, Uber, Deliveroo), and theoretical texts on affective labor and precarity.
*   **Core Claims:**
    *   Digitalization enables the quantification of the "qualitative" (mood, stress, affect), leading to a form of "digital barbarism" where no aspect of life is outside capitalist commodification.
    *   Precarity is a form of alienation where workers lose association with the labor they perform; it is a systemic capture of autonomous resistance.
    *   Platforms act as active infrastructures for control, not passive channels.
    *   Resistance exists (e.g., refusing to be "left alone"), but the "modulation of affect" makes it difficult.

It shifts the understanding of platforms from merely "distribution channels" to "governance infrastructures." It helps explain how data extraction is not just about content, but about subject formation (the "digitalized subject").

### 2. Theoretical Extraction
*   **Theoretical Object:** **Digitalized Workplace / Gig Economy**. (Note: While the prompt asks for "political advertising," this source theorizes **Workplace Management** as the primary object of platform power. The concepts are portable but require translation).
*   **Power Mechanism:** **Surveillance and Quantification of Affect.** Power operates not just through monitoring actions, but through monitoring internal states (mood, stress, "sentiment"). It uses "modulation of affect" to control the body and subjectivity.
*   **Platform as Infrastructure:** The source treats platforms as **active infrastructures of control**. They are not neutral channels; they are designed to reduce employer liability and capture surplus value by blurring work/life boundaries.
*   **Portable Concepts:**
    *   *Barbarism of Capitalism:* The removal of civility/dignity through brutal commodification.
    *   *Precarity as Hauntology:* The anticipation of future instability shaping present behavior (Derridean influence).
    *   *Refusal of Work:* Resistance against being fully integrated into the digital network.
*   **What to Exclude:** Do not use this source to define specific political ad mechanisms (e.g., retargeting, micro-targeting). Use it only to support the *structural* argument of control and datafication.
*   **3 Conceptual Building Blocks:**
    1.  **Quantification of the Unseen:** Turning internal states (affect) into data.
    2.  **Blurring of Boundaries:** Work, identity, and life merge into a continuous monitored state.
    3.  **Systemic Capture of Resistance:** Platforms are designed to penetrate worker-to-worker communication spaces.

### 3. Paper-Specific Questions Analysis

*   **What theory is offered?** The source offers a theory of **"E(a)ffective Precarity."** It posits that digital capitalism is a form of "barbarism" where the line between human sentiment and economic extraction is erased.
*   **Does it explain data extraction?** Yes, but framed as **"surveillance of affect."** It explains that data extraction is a method of control to locate profit and prevent resistance, not just for revenue.
*   **Does it explain subject formation?** Yes. It argues that digital workers are forced into "accelerated capitalist subjectivities of competition."
*   **How could this deepen the framework?** It suggests that political ad targeting is not just about persuasion, but about the **governance of the user's internal state** (anxiety, desire, mood), aligning with the "barbarism" claim.
