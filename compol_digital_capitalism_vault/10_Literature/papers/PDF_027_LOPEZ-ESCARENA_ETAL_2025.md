### **1. Bibliographic Identity & Core Content**

*   **Bibliographic Identity:**
    *   **Authors:** Ignacio López-Escarcena, Constanza Ortega-Gunckel, and María Elena Gronemeyer.
    *   **Journal:** *Social Media + Society*.
    *   **Year:** 2025 (Jan–Mar).
    *   **Article Type:** Research Article (Qualitative Textual Analysis).
    *   **Title:** "Is There Room for Connective Democracy? The Case of Chile in the Months Leading Up to the 2020 Plebiscite".
*   **What the Source Studies:**
    *   Examines the extent of affective polarization and "connective democracy" in Chile during the constitutional process (2019–2020).
    *   Analyzes user reactions (comments) to four specific political TV programs broadcasted on social media (X/Twitter and YouTube) leading up to the October 2020 Constitutional Plebiscite.
*   **Method and Corpus:**
    *   **Method:** Qualitative textual analysis (iterative, front-loaded coding). No statistical software used; Python script used for X/Twitter data collection.
    *   **Corpus:** 2,130 comments total (1,200 from X/Twitter, 930 from YouTube).
    *   **Stimuli:** Comments on four TV programs: *Tolerancia cero*, *Pauta libre*, *A esta hora se improvisa*, and *Estado nacional*.
    *   **Timeframe:** August–October 2020 (pre-plebiscite).
*   **Core Claims:**
    *   Even in a highly polarized context (post-2019 Social Outburst), user interactions on social media do not necessarily drift away from "connective democracy."
    *   Negative emotions (dislike, hatred) and negative trait perceptions are present but allow for reflection on the quality of connections, rather than precluding deliberation.
    *   Chile offers a relevant, understudied socio-political context for applying connective democracy theory beyond the US and Europe.
*   **Relevance to Diego's Paper:**
    *   Provides the specific theoretical definition of "connective democracy" applied to a Latin American crisis context.
    *   Offers empirical baseline data for the 2020 constitutional period, which is crucial for contrasting with the 2021 election (Boric's victory).
    *   Validates the use of TV/social media hybrid spaces as sites for political sentiment analysis.
*   **Cluster Tags:**
    *   #Chile #ConnectiveDemocracy #AffectivePolarization #SocialOutburst #2020Plebiscite #QualitativeCoding #PoliticalCommunication #TV-SocialMediaHybrid.
*   **Possible Citation Uses:**
    *   To justify the selection of Chile as a theoretical case study for digital polarization.
    *   To define the operationalization of "connective democracy" in non-US contexts.
    *   To acknowledge the limits of the 2020-2021 transition in digital political studies.
*   **Limits:**
    *   **Temporal Gap:** Stops at the 2020 Plebiscite; does not cover the 2021 election or the stabilization of the new constitution.
    *   **Scope:** Limited to comments on *four specific TV programs*. Does not capture general organic social media debate or WhatsApp usage.
    *   **Method:** Qualitative only; no quantitative correlation between sentiment and actual voting behavior.
*   **Open Questions:**
    *   Did the "connective democracy" observed in 2020 translate into the 2021 electoral outcome (Boric's win)?
    *   How did the post-plebiscite formation of the new constitution affect the digital polarization levels observed in 2020?

### **2. Contextual Extraction (Chile & LatAm)**

*   **Country/Election Studied:**
    *   **Country:** Chile.
    *   **Event:** 2020 Constitutional Plebiscite (preceded by the October 2019 "Social Outburst").
*   **Distinctiveness:**
    *   Chile represents a unique theoretical case because it combined massive street protests (2019) with a digital-first constitutional referendum (2020) within a post-dictatorship context.
    *   **Party Fragmentation:** Characterized by a clear binary split ("Approve" vs. "Reject" the constitution), mirroring ideological realignments (Left vs. Right) rather than traditional party lines.
    *   **Candidate References:** References Gabriel Boric (Left) as a key figure emerging from the "repoliticization" of the 2019 protests.
    *   **LatAm Distinctiveness:** Highlights the lack of research on connective democracy in Spanish-speaking/LatAm contexts compared to the US/Europe.
*   **Platforms Used:**
    *   X/Twitter (4th most used for news) and YouTube (6th most used).
    *   **Contrast Case:** The source explicitly excludes WhatsApp (personal/private context) and contrasts the "second screen" usage during the 2020 plebiscite against the 2019 physical protests.
*   **Justification Sentence for Chile as Theoretical Case:**
    > "Analyzing the case of Chile from the perspective of connective democracy allows for a relevant exploration of how emotions and digital connectivity influence democratic processes in an under-researched cultural and political context, thus expanding its scope and applicability beyond Europe and North America." (p. 2)

### **3. Conceptual Extraction (Digitalization & Platforms)**

*   **Concept of Digitalization/Platformization:**
    *   Defined implicitly through **"Connective Democracy"** rather than technical platformization.
    *   Focuses on digital technologies as central to organizing citizen participation and facilitating open exchange of ideas/data.
*   **Actors:**
    *   **Users:** Commenters on X/Twitter and YouTube (politically engaged segments).
    *   **Institutions:** TV channels (TVN, CNN Chile, Canal 13, La Red), Government (2019 Piñera administration), Political Parties.
*   **Platform Framing:**
    *   **Channel vs. Infrastructure:** Platforms are framed as **active deliberative spaces** (channels for "connective democracy") rather than passive infrastructures. They are sites where emotions and interactions are analyzed for their impact on democratic cohesion.
*   **Geographic Scope:**
    *   Local (Chile), with comparative ambition to expand the theory to "other regions aside from the United States."
*   **Mechanisms Linking Platforms to Campaign Strategy:**
    *   **"Second Screen" Symbiosis:** Users interact with televised content in real-time or via later access, creating a feedback loop between traditional media broadcasts and social media reactions.
    *   **Mobilization via Digital:** Social media was essential for promoting political mobilization, especially during the curfew imposed by the 2019 Social Outburst.
*   **Bridge Sentence for Chile 2021:**
    > "While this study focuses on the 2020 plebiscite period, the patterns of polarization and engagement identified here serve as a critical baseline for understanding how the digital infrastructure influenced the subsequent 2021 presidential election and the consolidation of the new constitutional framework."
