### 2. Conceptual Extraction: Digitalization & Platformization

*   **Concept of Digitalization/Platformization/Datafication:**
    *   **Digitalization:** Defined as a "hybrid media system" where professional journalism is intertwined with social media interactions.
    *   **Platformization:** Described through the concept of "in-house technological infrastructure" allowing parties to "amass and curate large amounts of data" and use "virtual networks."
    *   **Datafication:** Referenced as "psychometrical profiling of the voters," "openly available in social media," and the creation of "databases."
*   **Actors:**
    *   **Primary:** Populist leaders, political parties/candidates, citizens (as active participants in dissemination).
    *   **Secondary:** Journalists/Traditional Media, Opposition (cyber-attacks), Regulators (implied).
*   **Platform Framing (Channel vs. Infrastructure):**
    *   **Channel:** Used for viral dissemination, emotional attachment, and specific tactics (Twitter for shaming, Facebook for anger).
    *   **Infrastructure:** Described as "in-house technological infrastructure" for data curation, psychometric profiling, and microtargeting. Platforms are treated as the machine that enables the "subversive" mode of campaigning.
*   **Geographic Scope:**
    *   Global/Comparative. Explicit examples: United States (Trump), Germany (AfD), Austria (Kurz), Brazil (Bolsonaro), Europe, and Latin America.
    *   Specific empirical studies mentioned focus on Austria, Netherlands, and Sweden.
*   **Mechanisms Linking Platforms to Campaign Strategy:**
    *   **Virality:** Exploiting emotional attachment, novelty, and personalization.
    *   **Microtargeting:** Using data to profile voters (Gibson & Roemmele).
    *   **Direct Engagement:** Bypassing traditional media to communicate directly with electorates.
    *   **Platform-Specific Tactics:** Twitter = "@name&shame" (pressure journalists); Facebook = activating anger/sense of injustice/cultural backlash.

### 3. Risks, Ethics, and Debate Framing

*   **Risks Named:**
    *   Polarization, uncivil messages, misinformation, opacity, "democracy at peril," erosion of the public sphere, erosion of truth ("facts have lost their meaning").
    *   "Subversive" campaigning: Intended misinformation, demobilization of opposition, emotional manipulation.
*   **Empirical Evidence vs. Speculation:**
    *   **Speculation/Theory:** The concept of "bullshit" vs. "lie" (Frankfurt) and the general claim that truth is irrelevant are framed as theoretical observations of discourse.
    *   **Empirical Evidence:** Referenced in the summaries of papers within the special issue (e.g., Jacobs et al. provide data on @name&shame strategies; Gibson & Roemmele outline the "fourth era" based on data-driven campaigning models).
*   **Microtargeting Inherent Harm:**
    *   The text distinguishes between "scientific" (aligned with traditional campaigning) and "subversive" (misinformation/polarization) modes. Microtargeting is presented as a *tool* that can be used for either, suggesting it is not inherently harmful but *how* it is curated and handled determines the risk.
*   **Platform/Campaign/Regulator Roles:**
    *   **Campaigns:** Responsible for curating data and deciding between "scientific" vs. "subversive" modes.
    *   **Platforms:** Provide the "in-house technological infrastructure" and network tools that tune communication to be "against the establishment."
    *   **Regulators:** Implied need due to "cyber-attacks from the opposition" and "challenges brought upon by the development of social media."
*   **Counterargument:**
    *   Populism is a "thin ideology" that remains constant regardless of the medium; social media is a tool that modifies *how* it is executed, not the core phenomenon itself.
*   **Ethics/Evidence Debate Framing Sentence:**
    *   *"The core ethical conflict lies in whether the increased capacity for viral, personalized communication constitutes a necessary evolution of democratic engagement or a dangerous mechanism where 'facts become a flexible tool to reinforce certain worldviews,' thereby turning the public sphere into a battleground for emotional polarization rather than fact-driven discourse."*
