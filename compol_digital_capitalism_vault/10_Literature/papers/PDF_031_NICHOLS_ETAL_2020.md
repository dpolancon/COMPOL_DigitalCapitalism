### Bibliographic Identity
*   **What the source studies:** Computational methods for identifying media frames (automated vs. manual analysis).
*   **Method:** Comparative analysis of three techniques: k-means clustering with NLP feature selection, Evolutionary Factor Analysis (EFA), and Structural Topic Model (STM), plus manual coding.
*   **Corpus:** Two datasets used for comparison: one "very focused" and one "extremely broad" (specific content not detailed in pages 1-5, but implies news articles).
*   **Core Claims:** No standard method exists for unsupervised frame identification; STM is most effective for narrow-scope datasets but returns topics rather than frames for broad datasets; computational outputs differ from manual analyst outputs; multi-stage hybrid methods improve interpretability.
*   **Relevance to Diego's paper:** Serves as a **methodological precedent** for using computational tools in political communication. It highlights the limitations of treating frames purely as topics, which is crucial if Diego's paper involves complex campaign strategy. It warns against "plug-and-play" models.
*   **Cluster tags:** #ComputationalMethods #FramingAnalysis #PoliticalCommunication #Methodology #StructuralTopicModel.
*   **Possible citation uses:** To justify methodological choices (STM vs. LDA); to discuss the "frame vs. topic" distinction; to validate the need for human intervention in coding.
*   **Limits:** Focuses on "articles" (likely traditional media), English context (Oxford), and general framing rather than specific platform dynamics or election campaigns.
*   **Open questions:** Can frames be fully captured computationally? How to standardize "frames" across different conceptualizations?
