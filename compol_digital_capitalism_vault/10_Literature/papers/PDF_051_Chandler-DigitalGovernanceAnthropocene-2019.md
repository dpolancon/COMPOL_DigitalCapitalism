### 4. Paper-Specific Questions (Answered)

#### [G6] DIGITAL CAPITALISM PLATFORM POWER
*   **Theory Offered:** The **"Correlational Machine."** This is a theory of digital capitalism where power is exercised not through structural control or causal engineering, but through the management of **emergent effects** via data correlation.
*   **Explanation of Mechanisms:**
    *   **Data Extraction:** Implicit in "Big Data approaches" and "sensing capacities."
    *   **Commodification/Governance:** Governance is the primary output; data is used to manage "problems" (emergent effects) rather than create value per se.
    *   **Visibility/Control:** Control is exercised through "sensing" and responding to the "actual," minimizing unintended consequences by avoiding deep intervention.
    *   **Political Subject Formation:** Implied through "Digital Objects, Digital Subjects" (book title) and the "actualist" notion that subjects are constructed as actual effects to be sensed.
*   **Deepening the Framework:** It deepens the framework by moving beyond "platforms as tools" to **"platforms as ontologies."** It suggests political power is less about *who* controls the data and more about *how* the correlational logic structures reality (emergence vs. causation).

#### [G3] PLATFORMIZATION DIGITAL POLITICS
*   **Definition of Digitalization/Platformization:** Digitalization is the shift to **"Digital Governance"** where the "Correlational Machine" supersedes traditional causal policy-making. Platformization is the infrastructure of "sensing capacities" and "data analysis."
*   **Mechanisms Named:** Correlation, Emergence, Sensing, Response, Actualism.
*   **Passive vs. Active:** Treats platforms/technologies as **Active Infrastructures**. They are not passive channels; they actively construct the world through "processes of emergence."
*   **Scope:** **Global** (Anthropocene context). The examples are international (UN, World Bank). It suggests a pattern that is transnational and systemic, not just local.
