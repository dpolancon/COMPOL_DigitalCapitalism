### Bibliographic Identity & Core Content
*   **Authors:** Marcus Maurer, Pablo Jost, Marlene Schaaf, Michael Sülflow, Simon Kruschinski.
*   **Journal:** *The International Journal of Press/Politics*.
*   **Year:** 2022 (Online First; Published Vol. 28(4) in 2023).
*   **Article Type:** Research Article (Empirical/Theoretical).
*   **What the source studies:** The instrumentalization of news media by right-wing populist parties (specifically the AfD) to generate visibility and public awareness through "deliberate provocations" and "self-scandalization."
*   **Method:** Two exploratory studies. (1) Input–output analysis of 17 deliberate provocations by AfD politicians in German news media. (2) Media database research and Google Trends data analysis.
*   **Corpus:** German news media coverage of the Alternative for Germany (AfD) between January 2015 and December 2018.
*   **Core Claims:** Populist parties succeed by intentionally violating social norms to provoke negative media coverage ("scandalizing"), which increases visibility and public awareness. Attacks on migrants/minorities generate more coverage than attacks on elites. Negative publicity is beneficial for new/unknown parties.
*   **Relevance to Diego's paper:** **Analogue/Precedent.** Provides empirical evidence on media-populism dynamics and the "oxygen of publicity" mechanism. Useful if Diego's paper discusses how populists exploit media attention, though this source focuses on traditional news logic rather than digital platforms.
*   **Cluster Tags:** #RightWingPopulism #DeliberateProvocation #ScandalTheory #MediaLogic #AfD #Visibility #NewsCoverage
*   **Possible Citation Uses:**
    *   "Maurer et al. (2022) demonstrate that populist parties can exploit norm violations to secure media visibility..."
    *   "While often framed as negative, scandalization serves as a strategic tool for new parties (Maurer et al., 2022)."
*   **Limits:** Focuses solely on German context (AfD); does not cover digital platform algorithms; does not discuss microtargeting or datafication.
*   **Open Questions:** Long-term effects of scandalization on party legitimacy; cross-national comparison of this "scandalization" strategy; the role of social media vs. print news in this dynamic.

### Risks, Evidence, and Ethics
*   **Risks Named:**
    *   **Media Instrumentalization:** News media losing autonomy to be used by populists.
    *   **Democratic Legitimacy:** Populists delegitimizing institutions while gaining visibility.
    *   **Polarization/Antagonism:** Use of "antagonism" between people and elites.
    *   *(Note: Manipulation/Privacy/Microtargeting are **not** discussed.)*
*   **Empirical Evidence vs. Speculation:** **Empirical.** Based on two studies (content analysis + Google Trends).
*   **Microtargeting Inherent Harm:** **N/A.** The source does not discuss microtargeting. It suggests that "negative publicity" is often beneficial for new parties, countering the idea that all negative coverage is purely harmful.
*   **Platform/Campaign/Regulator Roles:**
    *   **Campaign:** Active agents (make provocations intentionally).
    *   **Journalists:** Counteragents forced into an ethical dilemma (reporting vs. ignoring).
    *   **Regulators:** Not discussed.
*   **Counterargument:** Journalists may report to delegitimize populists, but populists benefit from the visibility anyway. Supporters are media-skeptical and discount the scandalization.
*   **Ethics/Evidence Debate Framing Sentence:** "That debate has not been extended into empirical research, yet we assume that right-wing populists’ deliberate provocations usually receive media attention and create additional media coverage that exceeds the amount of media coverage populists typically receive."

### Paper-Specific Questions (G3, G5, G7)

**[G3_PLATFORMIZATION_DIGITAL_POLITICS]**
*   **Definition:** The source **does not** define digitalization or platformization. It defines "deliberate provocations" as "deliberate violation of publicly accepted norms" by populists to gain media visibility.
*   **Mechanisms:** The mechanism is "instrumentalization of news media" via "scandalizing" (covering norm violations).
*   **Active/Passive:** Treats media/journalists as **active agents** (following "media logic," acting as "counteragents") but also identifies them as **passive channels** of visibility when they cannot ignore the provocations.
*   **Scope:** **Local/National** (Germany). It is not a global or comparative study.

**[G5_ETHICS_MANIPULATION_RISK]**
*   **Democratic Risk Identified:** **Media Instrumentalization** and **Legitimacy.** The risk is that media loses the ability to be a "counteragent" and instead becomes a tool for populists to gain visibility ("oxygen of publicity").
*   **Specific Concerns:** Primarily **Visibility/Polarization** (via antagonism). It does not focus on privacy, misinformation, or algorithmic manipulation.
*   **Fear vs. Evidence:** The source prioritizes **evidence** (Study 1 & 2). It explicitly states that the debate about media instrumentalization "has not been extended into empirical research," positioning this paper as the evidence-based response to the theoretical fear.
