## 2. Theoretical Object & Mechanism Extraction
*   **Theoretical Object:**
    *   **Primary:** Posthumanism, The Cyborg, Anthropocentrism, Digital Subjectivity.
    *   **Contextual:** Regimes of Private Property, Historical Inequality.
    *   *(Note: "Platform Capitalism" is in the Book Title but not explicitly analyzed as a mechanism in this excerpt; the focus is on the "Digital Subject".)*
*   **Power Mechanism:**
    *   **Ontological Power:** The ability to define what counts as "human" vs. "technological."
    *   **Epistemic Power:** Poststructuralist critique of the "rational subject" (dispersing authority to language/discourse).
    *   **Economic Power:** Regimes of private property that drive digital advancements (implied).
*   **Political Advertising as Platform-Mediated Infrastructure:**
    *   **Status:** **Not explicitly covered in this excerpt.** The text discusses "digital mediations" of thinking generally but does not detail political advertising infrastructure.
*   **Portable Concepts:**
    *   **Hybridity:** The erosion of boundaries (nature/culture, mind/body) as a historical fact.
    *   **Anthropocentric Narcissism:** The tendency of theorists to place themselves at the endpoint of an eschatology of existence.
    *   **Historical Midwifery:** History acting as the "midwife of ontology" (ontological claims made based on current technological moments).
*   **What to Exclude:**
    *   Exclusion of the "linguistic turn" (Heidegger, Wittgenstein, Foucault) as insufficient for digital mediation.
    *   Exclusion of "self-identical" human beings (autonomous subjects).
    *   Exclusion of "emancipatory purposes" that do not address historical continuity of inequality.
*   **3 Conceptual Building Blocks:**
    1.  **Ontological Critique:** Challenging the "innocence" of posthuman claims (i.e., that they discover a truth about existence).
    2.  **Historical Materialism:** Situating digital dualisms (nature/culture) within processes of dispossession and enclosure (capitalism).
    3.  **Political Economy of Technology:** Arguing that technological advancements are products of "destructive capitalist compulsions," not just neutral tools.

## 4. Paper-Specific Questions (G6 & G3)

### [G6_DIGITAL_CAPITALISM_PLATFORM_POWER]
**What theory of digital capitalism/platform power is offered?**
*   **Answer:** The text **does not explicitly offer a theory of platform power** in this excerpt. Instead, it offers a **critique of how posthumanism treats digital capitalism.** It suggests that "digital capitalism" is currently treated as a source of "emancipatory" hybridity, which Rekret argues is "innocent" of its own historical materialist roots (private property).

**Does it explain data extraction, commodification, governance, visibility, control, or political subject formation?**
*   **Explanation:**
    *   **Political Subject Formation:** **Yes.** It argues that the "posthuman" subject is an illusion that ignores the "rational subject" of post-structuralism and instead focuses on "digital mediations" which are ultimately conditioned by private property.
    *   **Data Extraction/Commodifications:** **Implicitly.** It mentions "digital objects" and "Big Data" in the context of "regimes of private property."
    *   **Governance/Control:** **No.** It does not discuss algorithmic governance or specific control mechanisms in the provided pages.

**How could this deepen the conceptual framework?**
*   It deepens the framework by warning against **"theoretical narcissism"** where the theorist assumes their own historical moment is the "true" moment of existence. It pushes the framework to ask: *Who benefits from the "hybridity" of the digital subject?* It demands a shift from **ontology** (what is the human?) to **politics of property** (who owns the digital tools?).

### [G3_PLATFORMIZATION_DIGITAL_POLITICS]
**How does the source define digitalization/platformization?**
*   **Definition:** Defined as "digital knowledge production" and "technological advancements" that enable "automation of cognitive processes" and create a "complex infrastructure."
*   **Status:** In this text, it is defined philosophically (as a shift in *existence*) rather than technically (as a shift in *infrastructure*).

**What mechanisms are named?**
*   **Mechanisms:** "Automation," "Enclosure," "Dispossession," "Hybridity," "Private Property Regimes."

**Does it treat platforms as passive channels or active infrastructures?**
*   **Answer:** **Active Infrastructures.**
*   **Evidence:** The text rejects the idea of the subject as "autonomous over and against the world." It implies the "world" (technology/infrastructure) actively shapes the subject. It treats the technological infrastructure as a condition of existence ("midwife of ontology"), not a passive channel.

**Is the pattern global, local, or comparative?**
*   **Pattern:** **Global.**
*   **Evidence:** The text explicitly references "entrenched global divisions in access to the rewards" and "historical structures of inequality." It frames the theory as applicable to the "current epoch" universally.
