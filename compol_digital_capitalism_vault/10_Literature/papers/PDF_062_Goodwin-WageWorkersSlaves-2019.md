### 1. Bibliographic Identity & Content Overview

*   **Bibliographic Identity:**
    *   **Author:** Peter Goodwin.
    *   **Chapter Title:** "Wage-Workers, Not Slaves: Reflections on Jack Qiu’s Chapter".
    *   **Book Title:** *Digital Objects, Digital Subjects: Interdisciplinary Perspectives on Capitalism, Labour and Politics in the Age of Big Data*.
    *   **Editors:** David Chandler and Christian Fuchs.
    *   **Publisher:** University of Westminster Press.
    *   **Year:** 2019 (DOI: 10.16997/book29.m).
    *   **Source Type:** Academic Book Chapter (Critical Commentary/Theoretical Reflection).
*   **What the Source Studies:**
    *   The source analyzes Jack Qiu’s concept of "iSlavery" (from *Goodbye iSlave: A Manifesto for Digital Abolition*).
    *   It specifically examines the labor conditions at Foxconn and the consumer relationship with Apple/Facebook devices.
    *   It critiques the metaphorical conflation of "wage-workers" with "slaves" in the context of digital capitalism.
*   **Method:**
    *   **Theoretical Critique/Reflection:** Goodwin engages in a Marxist political economy critique of Qiu's arguments.
    *   **Comparative Historical Analysis:** Compares 21st-century digital labor to 18th-century chattel slavery using Marx's definitions of labor power.
*   **Corpus:**
    *   Jack Qiu’s *Goodbye iSlave* (2016).
    *   Karl Marx’s *Capital Volume 1* (specifically "Results of the Immediate Process of Production").
*   **Core Claims:**
    1.  **Distinction of Status:** There is a fundamental economic and legal distinction between "wage-workers" (who sell labor power but own themselves) and "slaves" (who are property).
    2.  **Production-Mode vs. Consumption-Mode:** Qiu’s argument holds weight regarding "production-mode" (Foxconn factory conditions) but fails analytically regarding "consumption-mode" (consumer addiction to gadgets).
    3.  **Marxist Rigor:** Goodwin argues that conflating workers with slaves sacrifices analytical rigor for polemical effect, despite the "grey area" of coercion in labor history.

    *   **Low Relevance:** If Diego’s paper focuses on **political advertising**, **data infrastructure**, or **voter behavior**, this source offers little direct utility as it does not address digital infrastructure or political campaign mechanics.
