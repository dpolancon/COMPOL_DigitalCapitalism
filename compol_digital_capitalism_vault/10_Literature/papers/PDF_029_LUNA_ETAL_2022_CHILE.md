### Bibliographic Identity
*   **Title:** Much Ado About Facebook? Evidence from 80 Congressional Campaigns in Chile
*   **Authors:** Juan Pablo Luna, Cristian Pérez, Sergio Toro, Fernando Rosenblatt, Bárbara Poblete, Sebastián Valenzuela, Andrés Cruz, Naim Bro, Daniel Alcatruz & Andrea Escobar
*   **Journal:** Journal of Information Technology & Politics
*   **Year:** 2022 (Published online: 17 Jun 2021)
*   **Volume/Issue:** Vol. 19, No. 2, 129-139
*   **DOI:** 10.1080/19331681.2021.1936334

### Source Study Details
*   **What the source studies:** The relationship between Facebook usage and offline electoral segmentation strategies in Chilean congressional campaigns. It examines whether online platforms disrupt political equality or reinforce existing socioeconomic stratifications.
*   **Method:** Mixed-methods approach.
    *   **Quantitative:** Analysis of 9,980 Facebook posts from 78 candidates using CrowdTangle API and "Condor" dataset. Techniques include hand-coding posts, Named Entity Recognition (geocoding), Structural Topic Modeling (STM), and K-means cluster analysis.
    *   **Qualitative:** Ethnographic fieldwork (observation of campaigns on the ground) and in-depth interviews with candidates.
*   **Corpus:** 80 Congressional Campaigns (Legislative Elections).
    *   **Location:** Three districts in the Santiago metropolitan region (District 10, 11, and 13).
    *   **Timeframe:** August to November 2017 (Election Day included).

### Core Claims
1.  **Normalization over Disruption:** Social media campaigns do not disrupt preexisting political patterns but rather reproduce and reinforce them (supporting the Normalization Hypothesis).
2.  **Reinforcement of Segmentation:** Online campaigns mirror offline segmentation strategies. Candidates use Facebook to reinforce their specific electoral appeals (programmatic vs. territorial) rather than creating a new unified electorate.
3.  **Socioeconomic Stratification:** Social media campaigning is stratified along socioeconomic lines. Wealthy districts use Facebook for programmatic appeals, while poorer districts use it to advertise fieldwork.
4.  **No Equalization:** Facebook does not equalize competition; it favors established or dominant parties and stratifies along existing political and economic inequalities.
5.  **Strategy Integration:** Successful campaigns combine online and offline activities. Relying solely on intensive online campaigning leads to poor election results.

*   **Geographic Limit:** Restricted to the Santiago Metropolitan Region (Districts 10, 11, 13).

*   **Scope:** Focuses on legislative (congressional) candidates, not presidential campaigns.

*   **Candidate References:** References 78 candidates (congressional) across three specific districts. Mentions previous research since 2005 (Díaz et al., 2006).
