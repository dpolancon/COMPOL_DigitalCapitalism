### Bibliographic Identity & Source Overview

*   **Bib Identity:** Qiu, Jack Linchuan. "Goodbye iSlave: Making Alternative Subjects Through Digital Objects." In *Digital Objects, Digital Subjects: Interdisciplinary Perspectives on Capitalism, Labour and Politics in the Age of Big Data*, edited by David Chandler and Christian Fuchs. University of Westminster Press, 2019. Pp. 151–164.
*   **What the Source Studies:** The source studies the concept of "iSlavery" (digital slavery) within the context of 21st-century digital capitalism. It analyzes the conditions of enslavement embedded in digital objects (smartphones), digital labor (manufacturing and consumption), and the resulting political subjectivity.
*   **Method:** Comparative historical analysis (using slavery as a method/framework), conceptual framework construction (Figure 1), and empirical observation (Foxconn labor, social media activism).
*   **Corpus:** Empirical observations from Foxconn factory zones (China), transnational labor campaigns (Hong Kong/Switzerland), digital media consumption patterns (Facebook users), and historical legal precedents (trans-Atlantic trade, Bellagio-Harvard Guidelines).

### Core Claims & Relevance

*   **Core Claims:**
    1.  Digital objects (e.g., smartphones) have not emancipated but created new conditions of "iSlavery."
    2.  iSlavery exists in two modes: **Manufacturing iSlavery** (production, e.g., Foxconn) and **Manufactured/Consumption iSlavery** (data/attention labor, e.g., Facebook addiction).
    3.  Slavery is defined by "de facto conditions" (powers of possession/transfer/disposal) rather than "de jure status."
    4.  Platforms are "instruments of enslavement" via corporate algorithms and manipulation of social media.
    5.  Resistance is inherent to slavery; digital abolition requires collective resistance, creative memes, and picket lines.

It challenges the "emancipatory promise" narrative often found in digital political studies and provides a structural analogy (slavery) to explain the rigidity of platform control.
