**What the Source Studies**
The source studies the political economy of digital technologies through a Marxist-Leninist and Autonomist lens. It focuses on the conceptualization of technology (specifically digital machines) not as neutral tools, but as "fixed capital" within the logic of digital capitalism. It analyzes the relationship between data, labor, and social being.

**Method**
Theoretical reflection and secondary analysis. It engages in a dialogue between Marx’s *Capital* (specifically *Capital* Vol. I, Chapter 15) and Toni Negri’s interpretations of Marx’s *Grundrisse*. It does not employ empirical methodology but rather conceptual exposition.

**Corpus**
*   Karl Marx: *Grundrisse* (Fragment on Machines), *Capital* (Vol. I, Ch. 15: Machinery and Large-Scale Industry).
*   Toni Negri: Various essays on technology, appropriation, and the multitude.
*   Reference to Marshall McLuhan (Techno-optimism) and Martin Heidegger (Techno-pessimism).

**Core Claims**
1.  **Technology as Fixed Capital:** In digital capitalism, data is "objectified digital labour" that becomes "fixed constant capital" stored on servers. Circulating and fixed constant capital converge in Big Data.
2.  **Appropriation as Struggle:** Technology is not determined (neither optimistic nor pessimistic). Its character (domination vs. liberation) is shaped by political struggle ("appropriation").
3.  **General Intellect:** The development of "general social knowledge" is a direct force of production, central to the "social worker" and the "multitude."
4.  **Commoning vs. Exploitation:** Capitalism appropriates via exploitation (estrangement); a post-capitalist future requires "commoning" (real appropriation of social being).

### 2. Theoretical Object & Power Mechanisms

**Theoretical Object**
*   **Primary:** The **Digital Machine** (Hardware/Software/Servers) and **Data** (as the circulating object of this machine).
*   **Secondary:** **The Social Worker** (Immaterial labor producing data).
*   **Tertiary:** **Appropriation** (The political process of transforming capital into common goods).

**Power Mechanism**
*   **Expropriation of Data:** Platforms convert online activity (user labor) into data. This data is stored as **Fixed Capital**.
*   **Surplus Value Extraction:** Digital labor creates value (data) that is stored and owned by the platform (capitalist), creating surplus value extracted from the user.
*   **Control of General Intellect:** By monopolizing the "general social knowledge," platforms control the means of production in the digital age.

**Political Advertising as Platform-Mediated Infrastructure**
*   *Note:* The provided text does not explicitly name "political advertising."
*   *Inference:* Based on Fuchs' definition of data as fixed capital, political advertising is a **commoditized output of the digital machine**. The infrastructure (servers) stores the data (fixed capital), which is then circulated to create new content (ads). The "infrastructure" is the data storage itself, which dictates the visibility and targeting of political content.
*   *Theoretical Link:* Political ads are not just "messages"; they are a circulation of the "constant capital" (data) generated by users.

**Portable Concepts**
*   **Appropriation of Technology:** The transformation of tools of domination into tools of commoning/struggle.
*   **Data as Fixed Capital:** Treating stored data like a machine part (constant capital) rather than just information.
*   **Real Appropriation:** Distinguishing between capitalist expropriation (alienation) and social appropriation (commoning).

**What to Exclude**
*   **Heideggerian Analysis:** The text critiques Heidegger for detaching technology from capitalism. (Exclude this if focusing on political economy).
*   **Marx's *Capital* Vol. I specifics:** While referenced, the chapter is a reflection on Negri. (Exclude detailed exegesis of Marx's text if focusing on modern digital platforms).
*   **Old Technology:** The focus is strictly on the "Age of Big Data" and algorithms. (Exclude analog industrial machinery metaphors if keeping the focus digital).

**3 Conceptual Building Blocks**
1.  **Digital Fixed Capital:** Data is not just information; it is stored, depreciating, and accumulative capital owned by the platform.
2.  **Technology as Ambivalent:** Tech is neither inherently good (McLuhan) nor evil (Heidegger); it is a tool of struggle defined by who controls it.
3.  **Commoning vs. Exploitation:** The binary outcome of the political struggle over technology (Capitalist appropriation vs. Social commoning).

### 3. Digitalization, Actors, & Mechanisms

**Concept of Digitalization/Platformization/Datafication**
*   **Digitalization:** The process where human activity and intelligence are "crystallised as fixed capital."
*   **Platformization:** The convergence of circulating and fixed constant capital in the realm of Big Data.
*   **Datafication:** The objectification of "digital labour" into data, which becomes a commodity and part of fixed capital.

**Actors**
*   **The Social Worker:** The user whose online activity generates data.
*   **The Digital Capitalist (Platform):** The entity that owns the servers (fixed capital) and the data.
*   **The Revolutionary Subject (Multitude):** The collective capable of appropriating the technology for commoning.

**Platform Framing (Channel vs. Infrastructure)**
*   **Infrastructure:** The text frames platforms as **Infrastructure**. They are not just channels for communication; they are the "digital machines" that constitute fixed capital.
*   **Active Infrastructure:** Platforms are active agents of accumulation (extracting data) and control, not passive pipes.

**Geographic Scope**
*   **Global/General:** The theory is derived from global capitalist logic (Marx/Negri). It applies to "digital capitalism" generally, not a specific nation-state.
*   *Limitation:* It does not account for specific national regulations or local political cultures (e.g., Chilean electoral law).

**Mechanisms Linking Platforms to Campaign Strategy**
*   **Mechanism:** **Data Accumulation.** Campaigns utilize the "circulating constant capital" (data) generated by the user base.
*   **Mechanism:** **Visibility Control.** The platform controls the "fixed capital" (servers) which determines the reach of political messaging.
*   **Mechanism:** **Surveillance.** The extraction of "social being" (user data) allows for targeted political messaging (implied by the focus on "digital labour").
