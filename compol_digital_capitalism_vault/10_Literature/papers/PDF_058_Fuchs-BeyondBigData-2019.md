*   **What the Source Studies:**
    The source studies the onto-epistemology and governance of the digital age. Specifically, it critiques David Chandler's reliance on Actor Network Theory (ANT), New Materialism, and Complexity Theory, arguing instead for a framework of **Dialectical Digital Modernity** rooted in Hegelian/Humanist Marxism. It analyzes the relationship between dualism, instrumental reason, and capitalist domination.

*   **Method:**
    Theoretical reflection and conceptual critique. It engages in meta-analysis of other theoretical approaches (Chandler, Latour, Haraway, Harvey, Habermas, Hegel) to construct an alternative ontological vision. It is a conceptual/theoretical intervention rather than an empirical study.

*   **Core Claims:**
    1.  **Critique of Dualism:** Postmodern and New Materialist approaches (e.g., ANT) that seek to "break through dualism" often inadvertently serve flexible accumulation and capitalism.
    2.  **Dialectical Modernity:** Modernity is not just domination; it is an "unfinished project" containing contradictions that allow for resistance and post-capitalism.
    3.  **Marxist Dialectics over Complexity:** Hegelian/Humanist Marxism (specifically the logic of *Aufhebung* or sublation) provides a superior framework for understanding complexity and emergence compared to "postmodern" complexity theory.
    4.  **Instrumental Reason:** Big Data and digital governance are rooted in instrumental reason, which creates crises that capitalism cannot solve, necessitating an alternative modernity.

### 2. Theoretical Object & Power Mechanisms

*   **Theoretical Object:**
    **Dialectical Digital Modernity** (vs. Big Data Capitalism / Postmodernism). The object of study is the *logic of governance* and *onto-epistemology* in the digital age.

*   **Power Mechanism:**
    **Instrumental Reason:** The primary mechanism is the application of instrumental reason (dualism, reductionism, determinism) which enables capitalist domination and creates crises. Power is exercised through the creation of "correlational machines" (Big Data) that govern society through co-relation rather than causal certainty.

*   **Political Advertising as Platform-Mediated Infrastructure:**
    *   *Note:* The provided text **does not explicitly discuss political advertising**.
    *   *Theoretical Proxy:* It frames **Digital Governance** broadly. If applied to political advertising, the source would theorize it not as a passive channel, but as an extension of "Instrumental Reason" and "Dualistic Logic" that participates in the "correlational machines" of sensing and seeing the world.
    *   *Infrastructure:* Platforms are viewed as part of the "unfinished project of modernity" used for domination from above (capitalist accumulation) and resistance.

*   **Portable Concepts:**
    1.  **Instrumental Reason:** The drive to calculate and control that creates contradictions.
    2.  **Dialectical Sublation (*Aufhebung*):** The process where contradictions are negated to create new emergent properties (complexity).
    3.  **Unfinished Modernity:** The idea that digital systems are not static but dynamic, contradictory, and capable of transformation.

*   **What to Exclude:**
    *   Postmodernism (viewed as an ideology of flexible accumulation).
    *   Actor Network Theory (ANT) (viewed as insufficiently critical of power).
    *   Complexity Theory (viewed as a manifestation of instrumental reason rather than a true alternative).

*   **3 Conceptual Building Blocks:**
    1.  **Critique of Postmodernism:** Rejecting the idea that "breaking binaries" is the goal; instead, analyzing the contradictions *within* modernity.
    2.  **Dialectical Logic:** Understanding digital systems as "fractal triangles" of subject/object change and continuity.
    3.  **Humanist Marxism:** Placing human emancipation and "praxis" at the center of digital development, rather than "non-human actants."
