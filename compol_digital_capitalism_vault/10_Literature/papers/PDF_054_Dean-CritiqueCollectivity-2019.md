    *   **Book Title:** *Digital Objects, Digital Subjects: Interdisciplinary Perspectives on Capitalism, Labour and Politics in the Age of Big Data*
    *   **Editors:** David Chandler and Christian Fuchs
    *   **Publisher:** University of Westminster Press

*   **What the Source Studies:** The study of digital activism, critique, and the political subject within the context of "communicative capitalism." It specifically interrogates the relationship between digital networks, the formation of the political subject, and the efficacy of activism/critique in the age of Big Data.
*   **Method and Corpus:**
    *   **Method:** Theoretical-philosophical synthesis and comparative political theory.
    *   **Corpus:** Lacanian psychoanalysis (Lacan, Žižek, Dolar), Alain Badiou (subjectivation), Elias Canetti (crowds), Antonio Negri and Michael Hardt (multitude/biopolitics), and contemporary digital activism practices (Twitter, Facebook).
*   **Core Claims:**
    *   "Digital" marks a specific period of capitalist development ("communicative capitalism") rather than merely a technological medium.
    *   The political subject is neither fully individual nor fully collective in the way digital activism suggests; it is constituted by gaps in structure and interventions.
    *   Current digital activism often confuses "crowds" (egalitarian discharge) with the "political subject."
    *   Networked platforms rely on power law distributions, creating a "dual problem of the one versus the many."
    *   Revolutionary politics still requires a "party form" or organized instrument, contradicting Hardt & Negri's purely networked theory.

It offers a counter-argument to networked optimism (Hardt & Negri) that may support Diego's argument for structural change or specific organizational forms.

    *   To define the "digital" not as technology but as a capitalist mode of production.

### 2. Theoretical Extraction

*   **Theoretical Object:** Communicative Capitalism (a specific period of capitalism where communication is commodified and central).
*   **Power Mechanism:** The power mechanism is the **blockage of the political subject's appearance** through the concentration and aggregation of data/communication, while simultaneously creating "opportunities for the subject's appearing" through crowd dynamics. It operates via "power law distributions of links."
*   **Political Advertising as Infrastructure:** *Not explicitly theorized in the provided text.* The text refers generally to "personalised mass communication" and "production of the devices," but does not analyze political advertising as a distinct infrastructure.
*   **Portable Concepts:**
    *   **Communicative Capitalism:** Capitalism defined by the commodification of communication.
    *   **The Subject (Gap):** The political subject is a gap in the structure (Lacanian/Žižek) requiring intervention (Badiou).
    *   **Egalitarian Discharge:** The effect of crowds (Canetti) which is retroactively attributed to a "people."
*   **What to Exclude:**
    *   Identity politics (Dean views identity as a site of politics lacking solidary associations).
    *   Hardt & Negri's "networked biopolitics" (treated as a counter-example to be critiqued regarding the need for the party).
    *   Bourgeois categories of information (Dean aligns with Christian Fuchs' Marxist categories of labor/production).
*   **3 Conceptual Building Blocks:**
    1.  **The Context:** Communicative Capitalism (Big Data setting).
    2.  **The Subject:** The political subject as a gap/intervention (not just individual or crowd).
    3.  **The Organization:** The necessity of the Party Form versus the networked multitude.

### 3. Concept of Digitalization/Platformization

*   **Concept of Digitalization/Platformization:** Dean defines the "digital" primarily as a **periodization of capitalism** ("communicative capitalism") rather than a neutral technological layer. It marks a shift in how capitalism determines the setting.
*   **Actors:** Activists, theorists, students, "people as the rest of us," and the "subject of politics."
*   **Platform Framing:** Platforms are framed as **active infrastructure/settings** ("general setting of communicative capitalism," "production of the devices") rather than passive channels. They are the "survivors or systems" that activists navigate.
*   **Geographic Scope:** Global/General ("Big Data," "networked media"), though the specific empirical text provided is general theory.
*   **Mechanisms Linking Platforms to Campaign Strategy:**
    *   Reliance on "complex networks."
    *   "Power law distributions of links."
    *   "Personalised mass communication."
    *   Mobilization of crowds that retroactively create a subject.
