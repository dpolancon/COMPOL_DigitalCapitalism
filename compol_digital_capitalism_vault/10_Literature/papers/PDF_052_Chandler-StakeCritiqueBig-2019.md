### 1. Bibliographic Identity & Core Analysis

**Bibliographic Identity**
*   **Source:** Chandler, D. (2019). *What is at Stake in the Critique of Big Data? Reflections on Christian Fuchs’s Chapter*. In *Digital Objects, Digital Subjects: Interdisciplinary Perspectives on Capitalism, Labour and Politics in the Age of Big Data* (pp. 73–79). London: University of Westminster Press.
*   **Context:** JSTOR Digital Archive. Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License.
*   **What the Source Studies:** A critical theoretical reflection on Christian Fuchs’s analysis of "Big Data capitalism," situated within the lineage of the Frankfurt School (Adorno & Horkheimer) and Critical Marxism.
*   **Method:** Theoretical critique, comparative conceptual analysis (Frankfurt School vs. Big Data Capitalism), and epistemological distinction regarding "data" vs. "datafication."
*   **Core Claims:**
    *   Big Data capitalism represents the "apogee" of dehumanizing modernity and the "Enlightenment project" (reduction to equivalence).
    *   It operates through **datafication**: making interactions visible and governable via correlation, rather than through causal laws or hidden abstractions.
    *   It functions as a new form of totalitarian controlling ideology where algorithms automate human decisions without ethics/morals.
    *   Chandler critiques Fuchs for reducing Big Data to mere "data" (modernist abstraction), whereas Chandler argues Big Data claims to access the "actual" via surface-level visibility.
*   **Relevance to Diego's Paper:**
    *   Provides a theoretical distinction between "modernist abstraction" and "datafication" which can deepen the analysis of **platform governance**.
    *   Supports the argument that platforms are not just channels but active **infrastructures of surveillance** (G3).
    *   Offers a framework for understanding **commodification** and **commodification of interactions** (G6).
*   **Cluster Tags:** #CriticalMarxism #BigDataCapitalism #EnlightenmentCritique #Datafication #SurveillanceSociety #AlgorithmicPower #PoliticalEconomyOfData
*   **Possible Citation Uses:** To justify a critical approach to digital capitalism; to define the epistemological shift from causality to correlation; to critique the "Enlightenment" view of rationality in tech.
*   **Limits:**
    *   Highly theoretical; lacks empirical case studies (unlike Diego's likely empirical focus).
    *   Relies heavily on Frankfurt School critique, which may oversimplify contemporary platform dynamics as purely "Enlightenment."
    *   Does not address specific national contexts (e.g., Latin America/Chile).
*   **Open Questions:** How does "datafication" specifically alter the *temporality* of political campaigns compared to traditional media (as the text hints at "time slowed down")?

### 2. Theoretical Object & Infrastructure

**Theoretical Object**
*   **Primary Object:** Digital Capitalism / Big Data Capitalism.
*   **Secondary Object:** The Enlightenment Project (Modernity, Rationality, Equivalence).
*   **Power Mechanism:** **Equivalence** and **Reification**. The text argues that algorithms and market logic reduce the "incommensurable" to abstract quantities (equivalence), destroying dialectics and human qualities.
*   **Political Advertising as Infrastructure:** The source frames advertising not merely as a tool, but as the mechanism of a "huge shopping mall" where "humans are targeted by ads almost everywhere." It treats commercial logic as colonizing society, where algorithms calculate human needs instrumentally.
*   **Portable Concepts:**
    *   **"Equivalence":** The reduction of difference to comparable market quantities.
    *   **"Datafication":** The process of making unseen processes visible/readable/governable via correlation.
    *   **"Surface View":** Governing via appearances rather than causal relations.
*   **What to Exclude:**
    *   Do not exclude the critique of "Marxist Critical Humanism" as the counter-emancipatory force.
    *   Do not exclude the distinction between "data" (abstraction) and "datafication" (actualist/surface).
*   **3 Conceptual Building Blocks:**
    1.  **Enlightenment Totalitarianism:** The modernist drive to equalize and commodify (Adorno & Horkheimer).
    2.  **Datafication Governance:** A shift from causal understanding to correlation-based visibility of the "actual" surface.
    3.  **Algorithmic Dehumanization:** The removal of ethics/morals from decision-making machinery.

### 3. Digitalization, Actors, & Mechanisms

**Concept of Digitalization/Platformization**
*   **Definition:** Digitalization is framed as the extension of the "imaginary of rationalist and bureaucratic control." It is the intensification of the subject/object binary.
*   **Datafication:** Defined as accessing reality by bringing interactions to the surface, making them visible, readable, and governable. It relies on **correlation** rather than causal relations.
*   **Actors:**
    *   **The Digital Machine:** Organizes Big Data, creates reification.
    *   **Capital/Labor:** Implicitly the subjects of extraction.
    *   **The Algorithm:** Instruments instrumental logic to calculate decisions.
*   **Platform Framing:**
    *   **Infrastructure:** Platforms are treated as the "digital machine" that organizes Big Data and constitutes a "surveillance society." They are active infrastructures of governance, not passive channels.
    *   **Governance:** Platforms allow the "modulation or regulation of processes" and the perpetual "warding off" of crises.
*   **Geographic Scope:** Global/Generalized ("Big Data capitalism"). No specific national focus in the text (unlike Diego's potential Chile focus).
*   **Mechanisms Linking Platforms to Strategy:**
    *   **Visibility:** Making processes "readable."
    *   **Correlation:** Using search terms to sense world processes (e.g., flu, conflict, shopping).
    *   **Modulation:** Using data to regulate/cancel out crises.
