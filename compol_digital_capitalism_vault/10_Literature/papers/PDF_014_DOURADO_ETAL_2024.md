### 1. Bibliographic Identity & Core Analysis

*   **Bibliographic Identity:**
    *   **Title:** Online Disinformation in Brazil: A Typology of Discursive Action of Harmful Political Content on WhatsApp and Facebook
    *   **Authors:** Tatiana Dourado, Victor Piaia, Viktor Chagas, Dalby Dienstbach, Marco Aurelio Ruediger, Eurico Matos, João Guilherme Bastos dos Santos.
    *   **Journal:** *International Journal of Communication*
    *   **Year:** 2024 (Vol. 18, pp. 2685-2709)
    *   **Institutional Affiliation:** National Institute of Science and Technology for Digital Democracy, Getulio Vargas Foundation, Fluminense Federal University.

*   **What the Source Studies:**
    *   Harmful political content and disinformation circulated by the "Radical Right" and "Far Right" during the **2020 Brazilian municipal elections**.
    *   Specifically investigates the "discursive action" (language in use) rather than just content truthfulness.

*   **Method and Corpus:**
    *   **Method:** Quantitative content analysis of digital interactions.
    *   **Corpus:** 3,503,540 messages spread across 1,676 public groups on WhatsApp and Facebook.
    *   **Sample:** A quantitative sample of 2,201 unique messages analyzed for specific discursive types.
    *   **Period:** 2020 Brazilian municipal elections.

*   **Core Claims:**
    *   **Typology:** Harmful speech is categorized into four discursive actions: **Uncivil, Conspiratorial, Hateful, and Dangerous**.
    *   **Platform Difference:** Harmful content was more prevalent on Facebook than on WhatsApp (attributed to Facebook's algorithm vs. WhatsApp's viral dynamics).
    *   **Political Link:** Messages explicitly about elections were associated with uncivil speech.
    *   **Right-Wing Strategy:** The Radical Right utilizes a mix of platform infrastructures (algorithmic/algorithm-free) to amplify inflammatory discourse during electoral processes.

*   **Relevance to Diego's Paper:**
    *   *Note: Context of "Diego's paper" is unknown.* Assuming Diego's paper focuses on digital campaigning, platformization, or LatAm political communication, this source provides empirical typologies of harmful speech and a comparative platform analysis (WhatsApp vs. Facebook) that could serve as methodological or theoretical grounding. It challenges the "fear" of disinformation by distinguishing between types of harm (e.g., dangerous vs. conspiratorial).

*   **Cluster Tags:**
    *   #DigitalDisinformation #RadicalRight #WhatsApp #Facebook #Brazil2020 #PoliticalCommunication #HarmfulSpeech #Platformization

*   **Possible Citation Uses:**
    *   Defining a typology for "harmful political content."
    *   Comparative analysis of closed (WhatsApp) vs. open (Facebook) platforms in elections.
    *   Empirical evidence of the link between radical right movements and digital discourse strategies.

*   **Limits:**
    *   Focuses only on **public groups** (misses private messaging or inauthentic accounts).
    *   Relies on quantitative coding of "harm" which may lack the nuance of qualitative intent.
    *   Does not explicitly measure real-world impact (e.g., actual violence or election outcomes).

*   **Open Questions:**
    *   How does WhatsApp's "algorithm-free" nature specifically affect the virality of disinformation compared to algorithmic feeds?
    *   To what extent do these discursive actions translate into offline political mobilization?

### 3. Risks & Ethics Extraction

*   **Risks Named:**
    *   **Manipulation:** Systematic attack on the electoral system.
    *   **Polarization:** "Inflame illiberal ideological tendencies."
    *   **Violence:** "Dangerous speech" capable of "massive violence" and "democratic breakdowns."
    *   **Misinformation:** Circulation of deceptive content without propagandistic purpose.
    *   **Privacy/Opacity:** Implicit in the "closed" nature of some group interactions, though less emphasized than algorithmic opacity.

*   **Empirical Evidence vs. Speculation:**
    *   **Evidence:** The study provides quantitative data on message volume and typology.
    *   **Speculation:** Theoretical claims about "democratic breakdowns" are framed as potential risks of "dangerous speech," not proven outcomes in this specific study.

*   **Inherently Harmful?:**
    *   The source distinguishes between **Misinformation** (misleading) and **Disinformation** (interested action). It suggests harm is not inherent to the content alone but depends on the **discursive action** (uncivil, hateful, dangerous). It questions the "fear" of misleading political content compared to other illegalities.

*   **Platform/Campaign/Regulator Roles:**
    *   **Platforms:** Viewed as active infrastructures that structure networks (some encouraging, some restricting).
    *   **Campaigns:** Utilize "grassroots" collectives, influencers, and "firehose" methods.
    *   **Regulators:** Platform standards tend not to address political content, allowing it to circulate more freely than illegal content (pornography, extremism).

*   **Ethics/Evidence Debate Framing Sentence:**
    *   "It is therefore necessary to examine the characteristics of the discursive action of political content used by radical Right counter-publics to understand the dynamics of harmful speech during elections."
