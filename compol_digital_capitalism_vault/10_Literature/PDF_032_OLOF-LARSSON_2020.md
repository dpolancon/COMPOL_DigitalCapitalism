### Bibliographic Identity & Core Analysis

*   **Bibliographic Identity:** Larsson, A. O. (2020). Picture-perfect populism: Tracing the rise of European populist parties on Facebook. *New Media & Society*.
*   **What the Source Studies:** A longitudinal structural study of political party and citizen activity on Facebook. It specifically examines the evolution of content formats (text vs. audio-visual) and the comparative engagement of populist versus non-populist parties.
*   **Method and Corpus:**
    *   **Method:** Quantitative longitudinal analysis using machine scraping/metadata analysis.
    *   **Corpus:** 1,194,567 Facebook posts from 225 political parties across EU member states, Iceland, Norway, and Switzerland.
    *   **Timeframe:** January 1, 2008 – December 31, 2018.
    *   **Tool:** CrowdTangle API.
*   **Core Claims:**
    1.  Audio-visual content (specifically native Facebook video) usage has increased significantly, while text usage has decreased.
    2.  Populist parties dominate citizen engagement (likes, shares, comments) compared to non-populist actors.
    3.  Increased engagement boosts algorithmic visibility, creating dependencies on platform functionality.
*   **Relevance to Diego's Paper:** Serves as a strong **analogue/precedent** regarding platformization and populist strategy. If Diego's paper examines platform strategies or populist political communication, this source provides empirical evidence of mechanisms (visual content, engagement loops) and a European baseline.
*   **Cluster Tags:** `#Platformization`, `#Populism`, `#VisualContent`, `#LongitudinalStudy`, `#Facebook`, `#EuropeanPolitics`, `#AlgorithmicVisibility`.
*   **Possible Citation Uses:**
    *   To establish the rise of video/visual formats in political campaigning.
    *   To validate the correlation between populist rhetoric and online engagement metrics.
    *   To discuss the methodological viability of analyzing longitudinal platform data (CrowdTangle).
*   **Limits:**
    *   **Geographic:** Limited to Europe (EU + Norway/Iceland/Switzerland); lacks Global South or North American (US) comparative data.
    *   **Temporal:** Ends in 2018; misses post-2018 trends (e.g., Brexit, pandemic impact, TikTok/Instagram rise).
    *   **Platform:** Exclusively Facebook; ignores other social media ecosystems.
*   **Open Questions:** How do platform algorithm changes post-2018 impact these findings? Does the "populist advantage" hold on algorithmic platforms like TikTok or X? What happens when "native" video is replaced by "short-form" video?

### Concept Extraction (G3 & G7)

*   **Concept of Digitalization/Platformization/Datafication:**
    *   The source explicitly references **platformization** (citing Van Dijck et al., 2018) as the "era of social media." It views the shift as the "fourth age of political communication."
    *   It focuses on **datafication** regarding the reliance on usage data (views, reactions) to inform campaign strategy and algorithmic visibility.
*   **Actors:**
    *   **Supply Side:** Political parties (specifically differentiating between **Populist** vs. **Non-Populist** actors).
    *   **Demand Side:** Citizens (measured via engagement metrics like likes, comments, shares).
    *   **Platform Actor:** Facebook (specifically its algorithms and native content features).
*   **Platform Framing (Channel vs. Infrastructure):**
    *   **Framing:** The source treats Facebook primarily as a **strategic channel** for campaign tactics (choosing content formats to maximize engagement).
    *   **Infrastructure Note:** However, it acknowledges **infrastructure** by noting "dependencies on platforms" and how engagement informs "algorithmic spread," suggesting Facebook acts as an active infrastructure shaping which messages reach voters.
*   **Geographic Scope:**
    *   **Regional:** Comparative European focus (EU + Iceland, Norway, Switzerland).
    *   **Note:** This source does **not** cover Chile 2021. It focuses on the period 2008–2018. There is no bridge sentence regarding Chile 2021 in this text.
*   **Mechanisms Linking Platforms to Campaign Strategy:**
    *   **Content Strategy:** Shift from text to native audio-visual content to leverage "spreadability."
    *   **Engagement Feedback Loop:** High engagement (visuals) $\rightarrow$ Algorithmic Boost $\rightarrow$ Increased Visibility/Followers.
    *   **Populist Advantage:** Populist parties utilize emotionalization and negative campaigning which resonates well with platform engagement mechanics.
