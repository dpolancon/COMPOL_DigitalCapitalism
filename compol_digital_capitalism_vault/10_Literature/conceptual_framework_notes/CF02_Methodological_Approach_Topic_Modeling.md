# CF02_Methodological_Approach_Topic_Modeling

## Theoretical Position (Fuchs/Digital Capitalism Lens)
From the perspective of Christian Fuchs, digital capitalism is characterized by the exploitation of users' data and labor, which is then commodified and utilized to generate profits for corporations. In the context of digital politics and political campaigns, this means that platform power dynamics play a crucial role in shaping the online discourse and influencing the outcomes of elections. As Fuchs argues in PDF_057, PDF_058, PDF_059, and PDF_060, the political economy of digital capitalism is marked by power asymmetries, where corporations and governments wield significant influence over the online sphere, often at the expense of citizens' rights and interests. Therefore, it is essential to adopt a critical approach that takes into account the class, labor, and exploitation dimensions of platform-mediated campaigning. By using Topic Modeling Methods, such as Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), we can uncover the underlying power dynamics and expose the ways in which digital capitalism shapes political communication.

## Literature Integration
The literature on Topic Modeling Methods (G2) and Venue Precedents (G7) provides a rich foundation for understanding the application of computational text-as-data methods in political science research. Studies such as Argote and Visconti (2025), Luna et al. (2023), and Carter et al. (2023) demonstrate the effectiveness of topic modeling in analyzing large-scale political texts and identifying patterns in user-generated content. Furthermore, the use of LDA, as seen in Carter et al. (2023), has been shown to be effective in uncovering distinct classes of user interfaces. The literature also highlights the importance of considering language-specific nuances, short-text handling, and validation strategies in computational research. Moreover, the work of Greene and McMillan (2020) and Kangaslahti et al. (2026) provides methodological precedents for using structural topic models and advanced computational methods in top political methodology journals. The use of multilingual word embeddings, as proposed by Wirsching et al. (2025), and cross-lingual text classification, as demonstrated by Licht (2022), also offers promising avenues for analyzing political texts in multiple languages.

## Empirical Implications for Chile 2021
The proposed framework element guides the empirical analysis of Gabriel Boric's and other candidates' Facebook ads in Chile 2021 by providing a methodological approach that can uncover the underlying power dynamics and expose the ways in which digital capitalism shapes political communication. By applying Topic Modeling Methods, such as LDA, to the Facebook ads data, we can identify patterns in the online discourse and track the evolution of issue salience over time. This can provide insights into how candidates utilize platform affordances to shape their campaign messages and how these messages are received by voters. Furthermore, the use of multilingual word embeddings and cross-lingual text classification can facilitate the analysis of Spanish-language political texts and provide a more nuanced understanding of the online discourse in Chile.

## Unresolved Questions
While the proposed framework element provides a methodological approach for analyzing digital politics and political campaigns, there are still several unresolved questions that need to be addressed. For instance, how can we ensure the validity and reliability of the topic models, and how can we interpret the latent dimensions of word embeddings and multilingual sentence embeddings in a political context? Additionally, how can we account for the impact of algorithmic changes on platform dynamics, and how can we generalize the findings to other political systems? Finally, how can we address the tensions between supervised and unsupervised methods, as well as between traditional statistical methods and modern NLP methods, in the context of political science research?
