# CF04_Democratic_Risk_Vs_Evidence

## Theoretical Position (Fuchs/Digital Capitalism Lens)
From a critical perspective grounded in Christian Fuchs' concept of digital capitalism, the integration of Ethics/Manipulation Risk (G5) and Chile/LatAm Context (G4) reveals a complex interplay between the political economy of digital platforms, the exploitation of user data, and the asymmetric power dynamics in digital political infrastructure. This perspective posits that the risks associated with digital politics, such as manipulation, polarization, and misinformation, are inherent to the capitalist logic of digital platforms, which prioritize profit over democratic values. As Fuchs argues in PDF_057, PDF_058, PDF_059, and PDF_060, the commodification of user data and the political economy of microtargeting are critical factors in understanding the democratic risks posed by digital politics. By adopting this critical lens, we can distinguish between dystopian fears of digital manipulation and empirically documented political outcomes in Latin America, highlighting the need for a nuanced understanding of the complex relationships between digital politics, public opinion, and democratic governance.

## Literature Integration
The synthesis of the Ethics and Manipulation Risk cluster and the Chile/LatAm Context cluster reveals a multifaceted landscape of democratic risks and opportunities in digital politics. Studies such as Anstead et al. (2025) and Boulianne et al. (2024) highlight the risks of manipulation and polarization in digital politics, while others, like Álvarez-Fuentes et al. (2024) and López-Escarcena et al. (2025), demonstrate the potential of social media to facilitate democratic participation and deliberation. The critical perspective of digital capitalism, as outlined by Fuchs, emphasizes the need to consider the power asymmetries and exploitation inherent in digital platforms, as well as the class, labor, and exploitation dimensions of platform-mediated campaigning. By integrating these perspectives, we can develop a more comprehensive understanding of the democratic risks and opportunities posed by digital politics in Latin America, particularly in the context of Chile and Brazil.

## Empirical Implications for Chile 2021
The framework element guides the empirical analysis of Gabriel Boric's and other candidates' Facebook ads in Chile 2021 by highlighting the importance of considering the political economy of digital platforms and the exploitation of user data. The study of Facebook ads can reveal insights into the use of microtargeting and the commodification of user data, as well as the role of platform capitalism in shaping electoral outcomes. By analyzing the ads through the lens of digital capitalism, we can examine how the capitalist logic of digital platforms influences the content and dissemination of political messages, and how this, in turn, affects democratic processes and public opinion. Furthermore, the study can investigate how the intersection of social media, populism, and democratic institutions in Chile shapes the electoral landscape and the democratic risks associated with digital politics.

## Unresolved Questions
The framework element still needs to address the tension between the optimistic views of social media as a tool for democratic participation and the more pessimistic views of social media as a platform for disinformation and polarization. Furthermore, there is a need for more comprehensive research that explores the complex relationships between social media, populism, and democratic institutions in Latin America, particularly in the context of Chile and Brazil. Additionally, the literature could benefit from more comparative studies that examine the similarities and differences between countries in Latin America, as well as more longitudinal research that tracks changes over time. Finally, there is a need for more nuanced research that takes into account the complex and multifaceted nature of social media and its impact on democratic processes, highlighting the importance of considering the political economy of digital platforms and the exploitation of user data in the analysis of digital politics.
