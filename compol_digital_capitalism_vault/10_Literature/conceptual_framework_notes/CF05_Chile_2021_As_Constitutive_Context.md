# CF05_Chile_2021_As_Constitutive_Context

## Theoretical Position (Fuchs/Digital Capitalism Lens)
From a critical perspective grounded in Christian Fuchs' concept of digital capitalism, the 2021 Chile presidential campaign is situated within a broader context of platform-mediated politics, where digital platforms such as Facebook play a pivotal role in shaping political discourse, influencing public opinion, and facilitating the exploitation of user data for political gain. This perspective emphasizes the power asymmetries inherent in digital political infrastructure, where corporations like Facebook wield significant influence over the dissemination of political information, often to the detriment of democratic principles. The political economy of microtargeting, a practice enabled by the commodification of user data, further exacerbates these asymmetries by allowing political campaigns to precision-target specific demographics with tailored messages, potentially undermining the transparency and equity of political communication. Moreover, this lens highlights the class, labor, and exploitation dimensions of platform-mediated campaigning, where the unpaid labor of social media users contributes to the profit margins of platform capitalists, and the exploitation of personal data for political purposes raises profound ethical concerns.

## Literature Integration
The integration of literature from cluster notes NB04_Chile_LatAm_Context, NB03_Platformization_Digital_Politics, and NB06_Digital_Capitalism_Platform_Power provides a comprehensive framework for understanding the 2021 Chile presidential campaign as a unique case study of platform-mediated politics. Studies such as those by Álvarez-Fuentes et al. (2024) and López-Escarcena et al. (2025) highlight the role of social media in shaping political narratives and facilitating democratic participation in the Chilean context. Meanwhile, research on platformization, such as that by Bossetta (2020) and Fuchs (2019), underscores the active role of digital platforms in shaping political communication, often through mechanisms like selective amplification of issues, disinformation, and algorithmic manipulation of visibility. The critical theoretical perspective offered by Fuchs, particularly in chapters PDF_057, PDF_058, PDF_059, and PDF_060, is essential for grounding the critique of digital capitalism and its implications for political campaigns. This includes the exploitation of digital labor, the commodification of attention, and the governance of political subjectivity through data extraction and algorithmic control.

## Empirical Implications for Chile 2021
The empirical analysis of Gabriel Boric's and other candidates' Facebook ads in the 2021 Chile presidential campaign, guided by this framework, would involve a critical examination of how these campaigns utilized platform-mediated strategies to reach and influence voters. This could include analyzing the use of microtargeting, the content and tone of political ads, and the role of algorithms in amplifying or suppressing certain political messages. Furthermore, the study could investigate how the exploitation of user data for political purposes reflects broader dynamics of digital capitalism, including the commodification of attention and the exploitation of digital labor. By examining the intersection of digital politics, platform power, and the specific context of Chile's post-uprising, constitutional convention, and pandemic scenario, this research could provide insights into the ways in which digital capitalism shapes political outcomes and democratic processes in unique and critical contexts.

## Unresolved Questions
This framework element still needs to address several unresolved questions, including the precise mechanisms by which digital platforms influence political outcomes in contexts like Chile, the impact of digital capitalism on the political economy of microtargeting, and the ways in which the exploitation of digital labor contributes to the profit margins of platform capitalists. Furthermore, there is a need for more empirical research on the intersection of digital politics, platform power, and democratic processes in unique contexts like the 2021 Chile presidential campaign. Theoretical tensions also remain, particularly concerning the balance between the optimistic views of social media as a tool for democratic participation and the more pessimistic views of social media as a platform for disinformation and polarization. Resolving these tensions and addressing these questions will require ongoing critical research and analysis grounded in a deep understanding of digital capitalism and its implications for political communication and democratic governance.
