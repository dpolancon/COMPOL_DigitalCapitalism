# CF03_Platformization_Of_Politics

## Theoretical Position (Fuchs/Digital Capitalism Lens)
From a critical perspective grounded in Christian Fuchs' work on digital capitalism, the platformization of politics is understood as a phenomenon deeply embedded in the structures of capitalist exploitation, where data commodification and the political economy of microtargeting play crucial roles. This perspective emphasizes the power asymmetries inherent in digital political infrastructures, highlighting how platform capitalism exploits users' data for profit, influencing political outcomes through targeted advertising and algorithmic manipulation. Fuchs' critical theory of digital capitalism (as outlined in chapters PDF_057, PDF_058, PDF_059, PDF_060) provides a foundational critique of how digital platforms, including social media, are not neutral but are shaped by and reinforce capitalist relations, leading to the exploitation of digital labor and the intensification of class inequalities. Therefore, the platformization of politics must be analyzed through the lens of digital capitalism, focusing on how it reinforces power asymmetries, exploits digital labor, and commodifies personal data for political manipulation.

## Literature Integration
The integration of the platformization of politics with the Chile/LatAm context reveals a complex landscape where digital platforms significantly influence political communication, electoral outcomes, and democratic processes. Studies such as those by Álvarez-Fuentes et al. (2024), Arnaudo (2017), and López-Escarcena et al. (2025) demonstrate how social media platforms are used in Latin American political campaigns to spread information, mobilize support, and sometimes disseminate disinformation. The critical perspective, informed by Fuchs' work, highlights the need to consider the economic underpinnings of these platforms and how they profit from political advertising and data extraction, potentially undermining democratic integrity. In the context of high digital penetration and structural fragmentation in Latin America, the role of platforms in shaping political narratives and outcomes becomes even more pronounced. For instance, the use of memes in Gabriel Boric's campaign, as studied by Álvarez-Fuentes et al. (2024), shows how digital content can create a sense of community among supporters, but also raises questions about the commodification of political engagement and the exploitation of digital labor in political campaigning. The spread of disinformation, as analyzed by Cazzamatta et al. (2024) and Pacheco et al. (2024), further complicates the political landscape, indicating the need for a critical examination of platform governance and the political economy of information dissemination.

## Empirical Implications for Chile 2021
The empirical analysis of Gabriel Boric's and other candidates' Facebook ads in Chile 2021, guided by this framework, would involve a critical examination of how platformization operates in the context of Chilean politics. This includes analyzing the role of social media in shaping political narratives, the impact of microtargeting on electoral outcomes, and the ways in which digital labor is exploited in political campaigning. By applying Fuchs' critical theory of digital capitalism, the analysis would also consider how the political economy of Facebook influences the dissemination of political information, the commodification of user data, and the potential for algorithmic manipulation of public opinion. Furthermore, the study would investigate how the specific mechanisms of digital politics in Chile, such as the use of memes and social media influencers, reflect and reinforce broader structures of digital capitalism, and how these mechanisms might be addressed through policy interventions aimed at promoting democratic governance and protecting user rights.

## Unresolved Questions
Despite the advancements in understanding the platformization of politics, several questions remain unresolved. These include how to effectively regulate digital platforms to prevent the dissemination of disinformation without infringing on freedom of speech, how to protect user data from exploitation by platform capitalists, and how to promote democratic governance in the face of algorithmic manipulation and microtargeting. Furthermore, there is a need for more research on the specific mechanisms of digital politics in different countries, including Chile and other Latin American nations, to better understand the regional distinctiveness of platformization and its implications for democratic processes. Addressing these questions requires a continued critical examination of the intersection of digital capitalism, political communication, and democratic governance, informed by theoretical perspectives like Fuchs' critical theory of digital capitalism.
