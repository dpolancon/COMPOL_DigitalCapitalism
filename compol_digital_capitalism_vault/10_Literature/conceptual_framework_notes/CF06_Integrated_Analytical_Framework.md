# CF06_Integrated_Analytical_Framework

## Theoretical Position (Fuchs/Digital Capitalism Lens)
This framework adopts Christian Fuchs' perspective on digital capitalism as the critical lens to analyze the intersection of platform capitalism, political crisis, microtargeting strategies, and democratic implications in the context of Chile 2021. Fuchs' work (PDF_057, PDF_058, PDF_059, PDF_060) provides a foundation for understanding the exploitative nature of digital capitalism, where users' labor and data are extracted and commodified by platform capitalists. This perspective is essential for critically examining the role of digital platforms in shaping political communication, influencing public opinion, and undermining democratic institutions. By positioning the literature in relation to platform capitalism, power asymmetries, and the political economy of microtargeting, this framework emphasizes the need to consider the class, labor, and exploitation dimensions of platform-mediated campaigning.

## Literature Integration
The integration of the literature from clusters G1-G7 reveals a complex landscape of digital politics, where platform capitalism, microtargeting strategies, and democratic implications are deeply intertwined. The study of paid digital ads (NB01_Paid_Digital_Ads.md) highlights the importance of understanding targeting mechanisms, ad visibility, and audience control in shaping political communication. The use of topic modeling methods (NB02_Topic_Modeling_Methods.md) provides a powerful tool for analyzing large-scale political texts and identifying patterns in user-generated content. The platformization of digital politics (NB03_Platformization_Digital_Politics.md) emphasizes the need to consider platforms as active infrastructures that shape the conditions of politics, rather than passive channels. The Chilean and Latin American context (NB04_Chile_LatAm_Context.md) highlights the importance of understanding the specific mechanisms of digital politics in different countries and regions. The ethics and manipulation risk (NB05_Ethics_Manipulation_Risk.md) cluster reveals a range of democratic risk factors associated with digital politics, including manipulation, polarization, privacy issues, misinformation, and unequal exposure. The digital capitalism and platform power (NB06_Digital_Capitalism_Platform_Power.md) cluster provides a critical understanding of the power dynamics at play in digital capitalism, highlighting the importance of considering the political economy of digital technologies and the role of algorithms and data in shaping political subjectivity. Finally, the venue positioning and precedents (NB07_Venue_Positioning_Precedents.md) cluster evaluates target journals, positioning precedents, and conceptual analogues for quantitative political communication research, providing a foundation for understanding the methodological and theoretical implications of digital politics research.

## Empirical Implications for Chile 2021
This framework guides the empirical analysis of Gabriel Boric's and other candidates' Facebook ads in Chile 2021 by highlighting the importance of considering the intersection of platform capitalism, microtargeting strategies, and democratic implications. The analysis of Facebook ads should focus on the targeting mechanisms, ad visibility, and audience control used by candidates, as well as the role of platforms in shaping political communication and influencing public opinion. The use of topic modeling methods can provide insights into the patterns and themes in the ads, while the consideration of the Chilean and Latin American context can help understand the specific mechanisms of digital politics in the region. The analysis should also consider the ethics and manipulation risk associated with digital politics, including the potential for manipulation, polarization, and misinformation. By adopting a critical perspective on digital capitalism and platform power, this framework provides a nuanced understanding of the power dynamics at play in digital politics and the implications for democratic institutions and processes.

## Unresolved Questions
This framework still needs to address several unresolved questions, including the impact of algorithmic changes on platform dynamics, the generalizability of findings to other political systems, and the interpretability of the latent dimensions of word embeddings and multilingual sentence embeddings in a political context. Additionally, there is a need for more research on the intersection of social media, populism, and democratic institutions in Latin America, particularly in the context of Chile and Brazil. The framework also highlights the importance of considering the complex power dynamics at play in digital capitalism and the need for a critical understanding of the ontological implications of digital technologies. Finally, there is a tension between the use of supervised and unsupervised methods, as well as between the use of traditional statistical methods and modern NLP methods, which requires further exploration and resolution.
