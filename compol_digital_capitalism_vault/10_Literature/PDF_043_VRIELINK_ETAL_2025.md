### 1. Bibliographic Identity & Core Analysis
*   **Bibliographic Identity:** Vrielink, J., Vliegenthart, R., van Remoortere, A., & Kruikemeier, S. (2025). *The International Journal of Press/Politics*.
*   **What the source studies:** The influence of targeted online political advertising on voters' perceived issue importance (agenda-setting) during the 2022 US Midterm and 2021 Dutch General elections.
*   **Method:** Mobile longitudinal linkage analysis (MILLA), combining mobile experience sampling (mESM), smartphone data donations (screenshot data), and content analysis.
*   **Corpus:** 2021 Dutch General Election ($N=103$) and 2022 United States Midterm Election ($N=31$).
*   **Core Claims:**
    1.  Exposure to online ads increases the salience (importance) of issues among voters (H1 supported).
    2.  There is no evidence that issue-based targeting successfully aligns ads with voters' pre-existing issue preferences (H2 not supported).
*   **Relevance to Diego's paper:** Serves as a critical empirical benchmark establishing that while targeted ads influence *salience*, they may fail to manipulate specific *issue preferences*, offering a nuanced view of microtargeting efficacy.
*   **Cluster Tags:** #Microtargeting #AgendaSetting #MobileESM #ComparativeElection #IssueSalience
*   **Possible Citation Uses:** To support arguments that microtargeting effects are subtle (salience vs. vote choice); to cite comparative methodologies (US/NL); to highlight the gap between targeting *data* and targeting *effectiveness*.
*   **Limits:** Small sample sizes ($N=31$ US, $N=103$ NL); limited to Western democracies; relies on self-reported mobile data.
*   **Open Questions:** Why did targeting fail on preferences despite success on salience? How do legacy media effects interact with these digital findings?

### 2. Technical Ad Extraction
*   **Paid vs. Organic:** Studies **paid content** (political advertising) explicitly, distinguishing it from organic content (news/articles) mentioned as a contrast.
*   **Platform:** Meta (Facebook walls, Instagram stories) and Google (news article edges, YouTube video starts).
*   **Targeting Mechanism:** Microtargeting based on "personal trace data" and voter groups with shared concerns about specific issues.
*   **Ad Visibility:** Placed across feeds, stories, news edges, and video pre-rolls; visibility is dynamic based on user usage.
*   **Disclosure:** Not explicitly tested in this study, but mentioned in the text as a general concern regarding "information asymmetry."
*   **Persuasion:** Focuses on **issue salience** (agenda-setting/priming) rather than direct vote choice or persuasion of specific party preferences.
*   **Segmentation:** Voter groups identified by shared concerns/interests derived from data.
*   **Audience Control:** Parties control message content; platforms control placement; algorithms match message to voter data.

### 3. Risks, Ethics & Empirical Evidence
*   **Risks Named:** Information asymmetry, detrimental to quality of political discourse, fragmentation of political agendas.
*   **Empirical Evidence vs. Speculation:**
    *   **Evidence:** Salience increase (empirically found).
    *   **Speculation:** Why targeting preferences failed (possible explanations discussed but not empirically proven).
*   **Inherent Harm Debate:** The source suggests targeting is **not inherently harmful** regarding vote choice (no effect found), but raises **subtle risks** regarding agenda fragmentation and discourse quality.
*   **Platform/Campaign/Regulator Roles:** Platform (facilitates targeting); Campaign (tailors content based on data); Regulator (implied need for oversight to prevent asymmetry).
*   **Counterargument:** Legacy media influence remains large (Langer and Gruber 2021); effects might be negligible if voters consume diverse political info.
*   **Ethics/Evidence Debate Framing:** *Targeting may lead to information asymmetry if different voter groups receive different messages, which could be detrimental to the quality of political discourse requiring shared understanding.*
