*   **What the Source Studies:**
    *   The emergence of the "Platform Party" (or "Digital Party") as a distinct historical type of political organization, analogous to the "Mass Party" of industrial society.
    *   The systemic reorganization of political parties to align with digital society logic (disintermediation, interactivity).

    *   Theoretical typology comparison (Mass Party vs. Television Party vs. Platform Party).
    *   Comparative case studies of specific formations (Podemos, Five Star Movement, Pirate Party, Momentum, Forza Italia).

    *   It is characterized by a dual structure: "Participationist" internally (online decision-making) and "Presidentialist" externally (Hyperleader + Superbase).
    *   It reflects the drive for directness, adaptation, and instant responsiveness found in digital culture.

### 2. Theoretical Object & Power Mechanism

*   **Theoretical Object:**
    *   **The "Platform Party":** A political formation that mirrors the architecture of digital platforms (disintermediated, interactive, personalized).
    *   **Digital Capitalism (Political):** Framed through the lens of "New Values of the Digital Society" rather than pure economic extraction.
*   **Power Mechanism:**
    *   **Disintermediation:** Removing the "apparatchiks" and bureaucratic intermediaries of the Mass Party.
    *   **Re-intermediation via Charisma:** Power is centralized not in bureaucracy, but in the "Hyperleader" (celebrity figure) who speaks to the "Superbase" (digital assembly).
    *   **Participationism:** Power is claimed as "participatory," but is actually managed through online decision-making platforms that favor the leader's narrative.
*   **Political Advertising as Platform-Mediated Infrastructure:**
    *   *Note:* The provided text frames communication more as **organizational infrastructure** (internal decision-making, candidate voting) than commercial advertising.
    *   Platforms (Facebook/Twitter) are used to build a "vast base of supporters," functioning as the *nervous system* of the party rather than just a billboard.
    *   "External communication" creates the "Superbase" identity.
*   **Portable Concepts:**
    *   **Hyperleader:** A charismatic, mediatised, celebrity-culture informed leader.
    *   **Superbase:** A highly activated, responsive digital assembly of members/users.
    *   **Disintermediation:** The removal of traditional bureaucratic layers.
    *   **Participationism:** The ideology that unrestrained participation is a force for good.
*   **What to Exclude:**
    *   The "Television Party" model (Berlusconi/Forza Italia) – treated as a transitional phase, not the target "Platform Party."
    *   Traditional parties using social media instrumentally (e.g., UK Conservative Party).
*   **3 Conceptual Building Blocks:**
    1.  **Historical Typology:** Industrial (Mass) $\rightarrow$ Post-Fordist (TV) $\rightarrow$ Digital (Platform).
    2.  **Organizational Structure:** External (Social Media Mobilization) + Internal (Online Decision Platforms).
    3.  **Power Dynamic:** The "Hyperleader-Superbase" duality (Polarized authority rather than flat horizontality).

### 3. Concept of Digitalization & Bridge to Chile

*   **Concept of Digitalization/Platformization:**
    *   Defined as a "restructuring of organisational forms... coherent with the nature of a digital society."
    *   Key attributes: Directness, Disintermediation, Interactivity, Adaptability, Instantaneous responsiveness.
*   **Actors:**
    *   **Primary:** "Hyperleader" (Top-down authority) and "Superbase" (Bottom-up digital users).
    *   **Secondary:** "Bureaucracy" (being removed/eliminated), "App Platforms" (Facebook/Youtube).
*   **Platform Framing (Channel vs. Infrastructure):**
    *   Gerbaudo treats platforms as **Active Infrastructure**. They are not passive channels for messaging but constitutive elements of the party's internal democracy and external identity.
    *   The party *resembles* the platform (e.g., Airbnb/Uber logic of customization and interactivity).
*   **Geographic Scope:**
    *   **Global/National:** Examples span Spain (Podemos), Italy (5SM), France, Northern Europe (Pirate), and UK (Momentum).
    *   *Note:* The text does not explicitly discuss Latin America or Chile in these pages.
*   **Mechanisms Linking Platforms to Strategy:**
    *   **Internal:** Online platforms for voting on candidates/policies.
    *   **External:** Social networks for supporter building and "instantaneous responsiveness."
