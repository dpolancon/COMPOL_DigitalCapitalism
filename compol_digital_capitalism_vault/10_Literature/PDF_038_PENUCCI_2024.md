### 1. Bibliographic Identity
*   **Source:** *Journal of Information Technology & Politics*, Vol. 21, No. 3, 2024.
*   **Title:** Resisting right-wing populism in power: a comparative analysis of the Facebook activities of social movements in Italy and the UK.
*   **Author:** Nicolò Pennucci.
*   **What the source studies:** The civil society reaction to right-wing populism in power through social media (specifically Facebook). It compares social movements in Italy (The "Sardine" movement) and the UK (Anti-Brexit/pro-European movements).
*   **Method:** Mixed-method research design.

    2.  Collective identity formation remains central in digital activism contexts countering populism, challenging the "connective action" paradigm that suggests individualization.
    3.  Social media platforms act as "battlefields" for contrasting interpretations of peoplehood rather than just passive communication channels.
