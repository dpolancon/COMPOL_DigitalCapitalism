### 2. Source Studies, Method, and Corpus
*   **Studies:** The source studies the intersection of digital labor, reproductive labor, and the "social factory." It specifically analyzes the economic and cultural logics of consumer labor (uploading data/content) within Big Data capitalism.
*   **Method:** Theoretical extension of previous work (*The Digital Housewife*), comparative historical analysis (Magdalene Laundries of Ireland vs. Big Data context), and conceptual analogy (Domestic work vs. Digital labor).
*   **Corpus:** Marxist feminist theories (Marx, Dalla Costa, Federici, Mies, Davis), Digital Labour studies (Fuchs, Tronti, Merteuil), and historical/carceral case studies (Magdalene Laundries).

### 3. Core Claims
*   **Historical Continuity:** The "social factory" (capital saturating all of existence) is not a unique feature of Big Data capitalism but has a longer history rooted in gendered and racialized labor (e.g., Magdalene Laundries).
*   **Reproductive Lens:** Digital labor is best understood through the lens of reproductive/domestic work, which straddles cultural and economic spheres, rather than traditional industrial models.
*   **Alienation:** Consumer labor (using platforms) results in the expropriation and alienation of cultural products and data, which serves as the cornerstone of surplus value.
*   **Platform Governance:** Platforms function as active governance mechanisms (similar to "pimps" or "agents") that broker exchanges, take cuts, and enforce rules, rather than passive channels.
*   **Intersectionality:** Understanding digital labor requires recovering histories of non-white, non-male, and subaltern labor experiences (women, people of color, LGBTQ+, disabled).

### 9. Theoretical Object & Power Mechanisms
*   **Theoretical Object:** Platform Capitalism, Digital Labour, Social Factory, Data Extraction.
*   **Power Mechanism:** Power is exercised through the **expropriation of surplus value** from unpaid consumer labor (data upload), **brokerage** between workers and clients, and **cultural/political discipline** (regimes of shame/normalization) enforced by platforms and state structures.
*   **Political Advertising (Infrastructure):** *The text does not explicitly theorize political advertising.* However, it implies that **data extraction** underpins the monetization of user attention. The "platform-mediated infrastructure" is framed as a system where user data is the "cornerstone of surplus value generation," suggesting that advertising is the economic engine driving the platform's ability to mediate political/cultural life.
*   **Portable Concepts:**
    *   **The Digital Housewife/Whore:** A conceptual model for digital labor that blurs intimacy and economy.
    *   **The Social Factory:** A framework for understanding where capital saturates life.
    *   **Reproductive Lens:** A methodological tool to analyze labor that straddles private/public spheres.
*   **What to Exclude:**
    *   Claims that private domestic space is a zone of "autonomous self-making" (Jarrett argues this is a myth for marginalized groups).
    *   The idea that digital labor is purely "socially meaningful" or "self-actualizing" without being exploitative.
*   **3 Conceptual Building Blocks:**
    1.  **Reproductive Labor:** Domestic/care work as a model for digital consumer labor.
    2.  **Surplus Value:** Data as the primary commodity for digital companies.
    3.  **Social Factory:** The saturation of all life (not just the workplace) by capitalist dictates.

### 10. Concept of Digitalization/Platformization & Mechanisms
*   **Definition:** Digitalization/Platformization is defined as the **"saturation of the whole of existence with the dictates of capital"** (reality of the social factory). It is not merely technological adoption but a deepening of capitalist accumulation logic.
*   **Actors:** Users (Digital Housewives/Whores), Platforms (Pimps/Brokers), State/Institutions (Magdalene Laundries), Capitalist Entities (Digital Media Companies).
*   **Platform Framing:** **Active Infrastructure.** Platforms are not passive channels; they are active governance mechanisms that "broker exchanges," "take a cut," "provide rules," and enforce "norms of the day."
*   **Geographic Scope:** Global/Comparative. While rooted in Marxist theory, the case study is Ireland (1922–1937), connecting historical Irish labor discipline to contemporary Big Data capitalism.
*   **Mechanisms Linking Platforms to Campaign Strategy:** *The text does not explicitly detail campaign strategy.* It links platforms to **surplus value generation via data extraction** and **governance via rules and norms.** (See Note on Chile 2021 below).
