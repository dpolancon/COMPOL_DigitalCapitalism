# Platformization of Digital Politics

## Cluster Definition
Explores the digitalization, platformization, and datafication of political communication, viewing platforms as campaign infrastructures rather than passive channels.

## Synthesis of Sources
The sources collectively define digitalization as the integration of digital data streams into the capital accumulation process, and platformization as the concentration of this communication within public page structures. They argue that platforms are active infrastructures that shape the conditions of politics, rather than passive channels. The mechanisms named include selective amplification of issues, spreading disinformation, creating a false climate, and leveraging algorithmic visibility. The sources treat platforms as active infrastructures that facilitate polarization, misinformation, and the manipulation of public opinion. The pattern is global, with examples from the US, Europe, and Latin America.

## Gaps and Tensions
The cluster literature fails to address the specific mechanisms of digital politics in different countries, such as Chile. The sources also do not provide a clear definition of digitalization and platformization, and the pattern of platformization is not fully explored. Additionally, the sources do not discuss the role of algorithms and data extraction in shaping public opinion and influencing political outcomes. Furthermore, the sources do not provide a comprehensive analysis of the impact of digital politics on democratic institutions and the political process.
