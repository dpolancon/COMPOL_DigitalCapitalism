# Chile and Latin American Context

## Cluster Definition
Investigates the Latin American and Chilean context, including elections, post-2019 uprising dynamics, constitutional processes, and regional campaigning distinctiveness.

## Synthesis of Sources
The cluster synthesis involves a deep analysis of the Latin American and Chilean context, focusing on elections, post-2019 uprising dynamics, constitutional processes, and regional campaigning distinctiveness. Álvarez-Fuentes et al. (2024) studied the 2021 Chilean Presidential Election and the use of memes in Gabriel Boric's campaign, highlighting the role of social media in shaping political narratives. Argote et al. (2025) examined the stability of ideological identification in Chile despite party system decline, emphasizing the importance of social identity in shaping voting behavior. Arnaudo (2017) analyzed the use of social bots in the 2014 Brazilian Presidential Election and the 2016 Impeachment of Dilma Rousseff, demonstrating the impact of computational propaganda on electoral outcomes. Arugete et al. (2024) investigated the role of local government and social media in managing the COVID-19 pandemic in Chile, highlighting the importance of mayors as trusted figures in times of crisis. Calvo et al. (2021) studied the impact of partisanship on health risk perceptions during the COVID-19 pandemic in Brazil, revealing significant differences in risk assessment between supporters of different parties. Cazzamatta et al. (2024) examined the spread of disinformation on WhatsApp and Facebook during the 2020 Brazilian Municipal Elections, identifying a typology of harmful political content. Chagas (2022) analyzed the use of WhatsApp in the 2018 Brazilian Presidential Election, highlighting the role of digital astroturfing in shaping public opinion. Dourado et al. (2024) investigated the relationship between tone and rhetoric in political tweets during the 2022 Colombian Presidential Campaign, demonstrating that negative tone generates more engagement than polarizing rhetoric. Gamboa et al. (2024) studied the impact of social media on political polarization in Colombia, highlighting the importance of tone and rhetoric in shaping public opinion. Ituassu (2023) examined the historical evolution of political communication in Brazil, emphasizing the role of digital media in shaping political discourse. Jamil-Marques (2024) analyzed the impact of social media on the 2022 Brazilian Presidential Election, highlighting the importance of digital platforms in shaping electoral outcomes. López-Escarcena et al. (2025) investigated the relationship between social media and connective democracy in Chile, demonstrating that social media can facilitate democratic participation and deliberation. Luna et al. (2022) examined the role of Facebook in Chilean congressional campaigns, highlighting the importance of social media in shaping electoral outcomes. Orchard et al. (2024) studied the determinants of media visibility in Chile, emphasizing the importance of institutional hierarchy and gender bias in shaping media coverage. Pacheco et al. (2024) analyzed the spread of disinformation on Twitter during the 2018 and 2022 Brazilian Presidential Elections, identifying a strong correlation between bot engagement and replies. Pallister et al. (2024) examined the impact of social media on campaign messaging in Argentina, highlighting the importance of policy orientation and attacks on opponents in shaping electoral outcomes. Pennucci (2024) studied the reaction of social movements to right-wing populism in power in Italy and the UK, emphasizing the importance of collective identity formation in shaping anti-populist discourse.

## Gaps and Tensions
The cluster literature fails to address the intersection of social media, populism, and democratic institutions in Latin America, particularly in the context of Chile and Brazil. While some studies examine the role of social media in shaping public opinion and electoral outcomes, others focus on the spread of disinformation and the impact of populism on democratic institutions. However, there is a need for more comprehensive research that explores the complex relationships between these factors and their implications for democratic governance in the region. Additionally, the literature could benefit from more comparative studies that examine the similarities and differences between countries in Latin America, as well as more longitudinal research that tracks changes over time. Furthermore, there is a tension between the optimistic views of social media as a tool for democratic participation and the more pessimistic views of social media as a platform for disinformation and polarization. Resolving this tension requires more nuanced research that takes into account the complex and multifaceted nature of social media and its impact on democratic processes.
