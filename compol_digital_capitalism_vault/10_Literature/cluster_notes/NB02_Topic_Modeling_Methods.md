# Topic Modeling Methods

## Cluster Definition
Focuses on computational text-as-data methods, specifically topic modeling (LDA, STM, BERTopic), preprocessing choices, short-text handling, Spanish language compatibility, and validation strategies.

## Synthesis of Sources
The literature on topic modeling methods in political science reveals a range of approaches and applications. Argote and Visconti (2025) used a mixed-methods approach, combining topic modeling with conjoint experiments, to study ideological stability in Chile. They employed an unspecified topic modeling algorithm on open-ended survey responses, identifying two main topics related to ideological labels and value judgments. The study highlights the importance of considering the social identity aspect of ideology in understanding voting behavior.

In contrast, Luna et al. (2023) utilized quantitative text analysis, specifically topic modeling, on social media posts from Chilean mayors during the COVID-19 pandemic. Although the specific algorithm used was not explicitly named, the study demonstrates the potential of topic modeling for analyzing large-scale social media data in political contexts.

Carter et al. (2023) applied Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) to open-ended descriptions of social media pages/sections, identifying four distinct classes of user interfaces. The study showcases the effectiveness of LDA in uncovering patterns in user-generated content and highlights the importance of considering the structural organization of social media platforms.

DiCocco and Monechi (2022) developed a supervised machine learning model for measuring degrees of populism in party manifestos, using a text-as-data approach. The study demonstrates the feasibility of automated text analysis for comparative measurement of populism across parties and countries.

Greene and McMillan (2020) employed a structural topic model (STM) on Scottish election manifestos to track the evolution of the constitutional question in Scottish politics. The study highlights the potential of STM for analyzing long-form political texts and linking topic models to voter behavior.

Kangaslahti et al. (2026) proposed Online Tensor LDA (TLDA), a scalable topic modeling method designed to handle large datasets. The study demonstrates the effectiveness of TLDA in analyzing massive corpora and highlights its potential for application in political science research.

Nicholls and Culpepper (2020) compared three computational methods for identifying media frames: k-means clustering, Evolutionary Factor Analysis (EFA), and Structural Topic Model (STM). The study highlights the limitations of computational methods for frame identification and emphasizes the need for human intervention in coding.

Osnabrügge et al. (2023) introduced a cross-domain topic classification approach, using supervised machine learning to classify topics in one domain (e.g., party manifestos) using a model trained on a different, related domain (e.g., parliamentary speeches). The study demonstrates the potential of cross-domain classification for analyzing political texts and highlights the importance of considering the transferability of topics across domains.

Theocharis and Jungherr (2021) provided an overview of the field of Computational Social Science (CSS) and its applications in political communication. The study highlights the potential of CSS for analyzing large-scale digital data and emphasizes the need for theoretical grounding in computational research.

Unlu et al. (2024) studied the dynamics of COVID-19 misinformation spread on Twitter, using a combination of text classification, BERTopic, and social network analysis. The study demonstrates the effectiveness of BERTopic for analyzing short-text data and highlights the importance of considering the role of social bots in shaping online discourse.

Wang (2023) compared the performance of Pretrained Language Models (PLMs) with traditional cross-domain topic classification methods for political text classification. The study demonstrates the superiority of PLM fine-tuning for achieving high accuracy in topic classification tasks.

Wirsching et al. (2025) introduced multilingual word embeddings for social scientists, providing pre-trained vector embeddings for 157 languages. The study highlights the potential of word embeddings for analyzing multilingual political texts and emphasizes the importance of considering language-specific nuances in computational research.

Zhao et al. (2024) studied the role of social bots in agenda-building during the Russia-Ukraine war, using a combination of time-series analysis and Structural Topic Model (STM). The study demonstrates the effectiveness of STM for analyzing large-scale social media data and highlights the importance of considering the impact of social bots on online discourse.

Licht (2022) proposed a cross-lingual text classification approach using multilingual sentence embeddings, demonstrating its effectiveness for classifying political texts in multiple languages. The study highlights the potential of multilingual embeddings for analyzing political texts and emphasizes the importance of considering language-specific nuances in computational research.

In summary, the literature on topic modeling methods in political science reveals a range of approaches and applications, from traditional LDA and STM to more recent methods like BERTopic and multilingual embeddings. The studies demonstrate the effectiveness of these methods for analyzing large-scale political texts and highlight the importance of considering language-specific nuances, short-text handling, and validation strategies in computational research.

## Gaps and Tensions
The literature on topic modeling methods in political science reveals several gaps and tensions. One of the primary gaps is the lack of studies that consider the application of topic modeling methods to non-English languages, particularly in the context of Spanish-language political texts. Additionally, there is a need for more research on the validation strategies used in topic modeling, as well as the interpretation of topic models in political contexts.

Another tension in the literature is the debate between traditional topic modeling methods like LDA and STM, and more recent methods like BERTopic and multilingual embeddings. While traditional methods have been widely used in political science research, more recent methods offer improved performance and flexibility in handling large-scale datasets and multilingual texts.

Furthermore, there is a need for more research on the application of topic modeling methods to short-text data, such as social media posts and tweets. While some studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of topic modeling for analyzing short-text data, more research is needed to fully explore the potential of these methods in this context.

Overall, the literature on topic modeling methods in political science highlights the importance of considering language-specific nuances, short-text handling, and validation strategies in computational research. Further research is needed to address the gaps and tensions in the literature and to fully explore the potential of topic modeling methods for analyzing political texts.
