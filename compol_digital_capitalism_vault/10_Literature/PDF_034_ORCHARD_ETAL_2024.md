### 1. Bibliographic Identity & Analytical Profile

*   **Source Identity:** Orchard and González-Bustamante (2022). "Visibility in the News: Politicians’ Presence and Prominence in the Chilean Press (1991–2019)."
*   **What the Source Studies:** Determinants of political actors’ media visibility (presence and prominence) within the Chilean elite press.
*   **Method:** Quantitative content analysis of political news coverage.
*   **Corpus:** Three legacy newspapers (*La Tercera, El Mercurio, La Segunda*) covering the period 1991–2019 (c. 30 years post-dictatorship transition).
*   **Core Claims:**
    1.  **Institutional Hierarchy:** Visibility follows a strict hierarchy led by the President and Cabinet, with Congress members lower.
    2.  **Gender Bias:** Female politicians have significantly less presence and speaking space than males, despite the system's stability.
    3.  **Conflict vs. Hierarchy:** While conflict frames boost visibility for non-elites, they do not disrupt the structural power hierarchy of the elite.
*   **Relevance to Diego's Paper:** Serves as a **foundational precedent** for understanding Chilean media visibility prior to the 2019 rupture; provides empirical baseline for comparing post-2019 fragmentation.
*   **Cluster Tags:** #Chile #MediaVisibility #NewsValues #Presidentialism #GenderBias #LegacyMedia #Post-Transition
*   **Possible Citation Uses:**
    *   To establish the baseline of "exaggerated presidentialism" in Chilean media.
    *   To validate the hypothesis that conflict frames do not override institutional power hierarchies.
    *   To contrast legacy press behavior with digital media trends in subsequent studies.
*   **Limits:** Data ends in 2019 (pre-uprising); focuses only on three legacy newspapers (excludes digital/social media); does not analyze the 2021 constitutional referendum directly.
*   **Open Questions:** How did the constitutional process (2019-2023) alter these hierarchies? How do social media platforms affect visibility compared to the legacy press studied here?

### 2. Case Context Extraction

*   **Country/Election Studied:** Chile (1991–2019). No specific election cycle is isolated; it covers the transition to the "second democratic wave" ending with the 2019 uprising.
*   **Distinctiveness:**
    *   **System:** "Exaggerated presidentialism" (strong executive, weak legislature).
    *   **Media:** "Captured-liberal" legacy media systems oriented towards elites.
    *   **Context:** Post-authoritarian consolidation vs. 2019 social mobilization.
*   **Chile 2019 Uprising / Constitutional Process:** The paper uses the 2019 uprising as a **justification for the study** (highlighting the rupture in the "poster child" narrative) but the data **stops in 2019**. It does not study the aftermath or the constitutional process.
*   **Party Fragmentation / Candidate References:** Mentions the transition from two coalitions (*Concertación*/*Alianza*) to an atomized party system from 2010 onwards.
*   **Platforms Used:** Elite newspapers: *La Tercera, El Mercurio, La Segunda*.
*   **Contrast Case for Chile 2021:** The paper does not study 2021, but implicitly contrasts the stability of 1991–2019 (the "poster child" era) against the crisis of 2019–2020 to justify the need for inspection of elite communication.
*   **Justification Sentence for Chile as Theoretical Case:** *"Chile was for decades considered the poster child of the region... For that reason, spaces of elite communication deserve inspection concerning their representations of politics and the visibility of political actors."*
