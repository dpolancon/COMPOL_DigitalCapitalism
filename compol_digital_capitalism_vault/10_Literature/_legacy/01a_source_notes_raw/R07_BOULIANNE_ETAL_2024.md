Vulnerable populations are structurally dependent on their local municipalities—which act as "street-level bureaucrats"—for basic welfare and survival.
- **The Limits of Democratization:** The study warns against the assumption that social media universally democratizes or disrupts politics. Because poor households rely on the local state for survival, strict COVID-19 lockdowns forced poorer citizens to rely heavily on platforms as an indispensable lifeline to access critical state services, whereas wealthier citizens, who rely on private self-provision, did not face this same digital vulnerability.

# N044_PDF007_P002


**1. Bibliographic identity**

- **Author(s):** Shelley Boulianne, Christian P. Hoffmann, and Michael Bossetta.
- **Year:** 2024 (online first) / 2025 (print volume).
- **Title:** Social media platforms for politics: A comparison of Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, YouTube, Reddit, Snapchat, and WhatsApp.
- **Journal:** _New Media & Society_.
- **DOI:** 10.1177/14614448241262415.

**2. Research object**

- The study analyzes the role of **political ideology in the adoption and political uses** (exposure to and posting of political content) across seven different social media platforms. It systematically compares how distinct "structural" (user connectivity, privacy, anonymity) and "social" networking features (audience, ties, norms) shape political behavior online.

**3. Case and scope**

- **Country/Region:** Four established Western democracies: Canada, France, the United States, and the United Kingdom.
- **Election/cycle:** Surveys were conducted in October 2019 and February 2021, covering the periods surrounding the 2019 elections in Canada and the UK, and the 2020 election in the US.
- **Sample size:** A cross-national online survey of n=12,302 respondents.

**4. Method**

- **Data Collection:** Online panel surveys employing quotas to ensure the samples were demographically representative of the population in each country based on age, sex, and education.
- **Measurements:** Relied on self-reported survey measures asking respondents about their frequency of platform use in the past 12 months, followed by questions targeting the frequency of seeing and posting specific types of political content (e.g., elections, policy debates).
- **Analysis:** Conducted correlational analyses and multivariate logistic regression models. The models controlled for sociodemographic variables (gender, education, age), country of origin, and political interest.

**5. Core claim**

- **The Funnel Process:** There is a distinct "funnel" in digital political behavior: large numbers of citizens adopt a platform, fewer report exposure to political content, and even fewer actively post it. Throughout each step of this funnel, **ideological differences widen significantly**.
- **Right-Wing Dominance in Posting:** Right-wing users are much more likely to adopt, report exposure to, and post political content. Right-wing ideology positively predicts posting on _all_ seven platforms. The authors explain this as right-wing users seeking alternative spaces due to perceived hostility in mainstream media.
- **Distinctive Platforms:** Ideological divides are strongest on Reddit, Snapchat, and WhatsApp. Closed network structures (WhatsApp, Snapchat) and anonymity features (Reddit) create environments that facilitate highly polarized political sharing, particularly for right-wing users.
- **Facebook as an Exception:** Facebook is the deviation; it has ideologically heterogeneous networks and serves as a general-purpose platform where users often avoid overtly political discussions unless triggered by strong social norms.

**6. Relevance to Diego paper**

- **Theoretical Framework (Platform Affordances):** Highly relevant for establishing a comparative framework. It breaks down "social media" into specific structural networking features (open vs. closed connectivity, privacy defaults, anonymity) that dictate digital political behavior.
- **Direct Call for Case Study:** The authors explicitly name **Chile** as a vital next step for testing their findings, making this an excellent legitimating source to justify Diego's regional focus.
- **Ideology vs. Structure Foil:** This paper establishes that in the Global North, _ideology_ (specifically left vs. right divides) is the primary driver of distinct platform behaviors. Diego can contrast this with his Chilean context (relying on Luna et al.), demonstrating how _socioeconomic and structural_ dependencies often override pure ideological variables in Latin America.

**7. Exact extraction anchors**

- _On the funnel effect:_ "Our large sample size allows us to document a funnel process where large numbers adopt a platform, fewer see political content, and even fewer post. In this funnel process, ideological differences become larger."
- _On right-wing platform usage:_ "Right-wing users are more likely to post on all social media platforms, which we explain in terms of discontent with mainstream media and a preference for platforms enabling closed, private discussions, such as WhatsApp and Snapchat."
- _Direct call for Diego's region:_ "Future research should include Latin American countries, such as Chile, Western democracies (Valeriani and Vaccari, 2018), and other countries to determine the broader relevance of these theories and findings."

**8. Limitations**

- **Self-Reported Bias:** The methodology is restricted to cross-sectional self-reports, which the authors acknowledge are inherently vulnerable to recall and over-reporting biases, particularly among active users. They note web-tracking data would be ideal to validate these behaviors.
- **Geographic Scope:** The empirical evidence relies entirely on four established democracies in the Global North. Consequently, its findings regarding the primacy of ideology cannot automatically be generalized to developing democracies experiencing institutional crises or severe structural inequality.

Based on the provided source (Boulianne, Hoffmann, and Bossetta 2024/2025), here is the extracted material regarding ethics, democratic risks, and manipulation:

**1. What democratic risks are identified?** The source does not focus on computational manipulation, algorithmic opacity, or misinformation campaigns. Instead, the primary democratic concerns identified are **echo chambers, political polarization, and the splintering of the public sphere**.

- **Echo Chambers and Radicalization:** The authors warn that the strong ideological sorting occurring on structurally closed platforms (like WhatsApp and Snapchat) and anonymous platforms (like Reddit) facilitates the creation of echo chambers. These ideologically homogeneous, like-minded networks pose a democratic risk because they can "facilitate extreme views, radicalization, and lower tolerance".
- **Splintering of the Public Sphere:** The study warns of a fragmented digital landscape where different ideological groups (particularly right-wing users) retreat to distinct platforms to find alternative communities. This splinters the public sphere, creating an imbalance in digital discourse that "limits opportunities to hear and understand the other side and settle on a compromise".
- **Uncivil Discourse:** The study also identifies that certain structural affordances, specifically the lack of identity verification (anonymity) on platforms like Reddit, might facilitate uncivil political discourse by lowering the risk of social sanctions.

**2. Does the source distinguish fear from evidence?** Yes. The authors move beyond generalized, theoretical fears about "social media polarization" by grounding their claims in **robust cross-national empirical evidence** (a survey of 12,302 users across the US, UK, France, and Canada).

- **Evidence of the "Funnel Process":** Rather than assuming platforms universally radicalize all users, their evidence demonstrates a specific "funnel process" of polarization. Large numbers of people adopt platforms, but ideological divides widen significantly when measuring who actually sees political content, and become most severe when measuring who actively _posts_ it.
- **Asymmetrical Polarization:** The evidence challenges the fear of symmetrical polarization by showing that this dynamic is disproportionately driven by **right-wing users**, who are significantly more likely to adopt, report exposure to, and actively post political content across these platforms (often as a corrective action against perceived hostility in mainstream media).
