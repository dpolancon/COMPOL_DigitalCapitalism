
# N001_PDF001_P00

Here is the extracted information from the source for your Obsidian literature registry:

**1. Bibliographic identity**

- **Author(s):** Mario Álvarez Fuentes and Claudia Mellado.
- **Year:** 2024.
- **Title:** Between Hagiography and Self-Trolling: Multimodal Analysis of Memes for Boric in the 2021 Chilean Presidential Election.
- **Journal:** International Journal of Communication.
- **DOI:** Not visible in the source document.

**2. Research object**

- The study examines Internet memes (images and videos) in political communication, focusing on how absurdity is used to create heroic narratives and build interpretive communities.

**3. Case and scope**

- **Country/Region:** Chile.
- **Election/cycle:** 2021 Chilean Presidential Election (specifically focusing on the week after the first round, beginning December 21, 2021).
- **Platform:** Social networks and personal instant messaging applications, specifically focusing on Twitter accounts such as _Shitpost Boris_, _Gamba_, and _The Clinic_.
- **Sample size:** A final corpus of 201 memes (180 images and 21 videos) filtered down from an initial 248.

**4. Method**

- Qualitative, specifically a multimodal visual discourse analysis based on Kress and Van Leeuwen's meta-functions (ideational, interpersonal, and textual) combined with inductive thematic analysis.

**5. Core claim**

- Supporters of Gabriel Boric used absurd memes to construct a "hagiography"—an idealized, heroic narrative portraying him as a savior rescuing Chilean democracy from the threat of _Pinochetismo_.
- Rather than persuading opponents, these memes functioned to forge an intimate, in-group "interpretive community" among supporters.
- Users engaged in "massive self-trolling," mocking their own resignation and framing their support for Boric as a necessary compromise for an unsatisfactory or "yellow" (insufficiently leftist) candidate.
- The meme narrative framed the election as a climactic moment where Boric was aided by former heroes (athletes, older politicians) and supported by marginalized "underdog" groups.

**6. Relevance to Diego paper**

- **Chile/LatAm:** Highly relevant. It provides a detailed qualitative case study of the digital culture surrounding Gabriel Boric and José Antonio Kast during the 2021 Chilean presidential runoff.
- **Digitalization/platformization:** Relevant. It discusses memes as a form of "mass self-communication" over horizontal digital networks, highlighting how users leverage participatory memetic culture to re-signify political events and form communities.
- _Not relevant for:_ Paid ads, microtargeting, LDA/topic modeling, or ethics/regulation, as the study relies exclusively on qualitative visual discourse analysis.

**7. Exact extraction anchors**

- **On meme functions:** "Internet memes lend themselves to at least two functions in politics: re-signifying political discourses and creating interpretive communities" (Qualitative Multimodal Analysis).
- **On the Boric narrative:** "...users built an absurd account of the election, portraying Boric as the principled hero coming to save the day from the resurgence of Pinochetismo, and they trolled themselves for having to settle for a not-that-leftist candidate" (Introduction).
- **On self-trolling & community:** "...Boric’s hagiographic idealization can be viewed as a form of mass self-trolling—self-generated, self-directed, and self-selected— primarily for Boric’s community of followers" (Discussion).
- **On political resignation:** "This self-mockery in memes suggests a critical distance from supporting an unsatisfactory candidate" (Discussion).

**8. Limitations**

- The findings are based strictly on textual and visual interpretation; the analysis "does not account for the creators' actual intentions".
- The sampling method created an "additional bias beyond electoral support" because the curated Twitter accounts actively filtered out non-humorous campaign materials.
- The persuasive effects of the memes on undecided voters or political opponents cannot be inferred, as the data indicates the memes were geared toward an existing "community of converts".
