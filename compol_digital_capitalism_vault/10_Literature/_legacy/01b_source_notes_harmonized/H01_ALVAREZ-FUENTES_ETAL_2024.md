## What this source is about

This study looks at paid Facebook ads during the 2017 UK General Election and compares them to traditional TV ads. It explains that because Facebook ads appear in "private" feeds, they are much less transparent and accountable than public broadcasts.

## Method or approach

The researchers compared a dataset of Facebook ads (gathered from a browser plugin called "Who Targets Me") against traditional Party Election Broadcasts (PEBs).

## Case, country, platform, and period

Country / region: United Kingdom
Election or political context: 2017 General Election
Platform: Facebook
Time period: 2017
Type of material studied: Paid digital advertisements and traditional broadcast ads.

## Main relevance for Diego’s paper

This is a core source for studying **Paid Ads (G1)** and **Democratic Risk (G5)**. It provides evidence of "political redlining"—where certain voters are ignored by campaigns—and shows how platform algorithms act as "active infrastructures" that shape political strategy.

## Useful claims

- Facebook advertising is "nontransparent" because it happens in private individual feeds rather than public spaces.
- Campaigns use the privacy of Facebook to run negative or contradictory messages they wouldn't use on TV.
- Platform pricing structures make it cheaper to mobilize existing supporters than to try to persuade new ones, which can increase polarization.
- Algorithms act as "hidden arbiters" that can misdirect ads or exclude specific demographic groups regardless of the campaign's intent.
- Political "redlining" is a risk where citizens deemed to have "low electoral value" are excluded from democratic communication.
