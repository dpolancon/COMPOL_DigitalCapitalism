## Basic information

Title: Causes and Consequences of Ideological Persistence: The Case of Chile
Journal / book / source: Latin American Politics and Society
PDF filename: PDF003
Source ID: PDF003

## What this source is about

This study looks at why Chilean voters still identify strongly as "Left" or "Right" even though they don't trust political parties anymore. It argues that ideology has become a "social identity" that people hold onto, partly because of the lasting impact of the 1988 plebiscite. It uses a mix of methods, including a computer technique (LDA) to analyze what people think of when they hear "Left" or "Right."

## Method or approach

Mixed-methods: 
1. **Topic Modeling (LDA):** Unsupervised analysis of short, open-ended survey responses (k=2).
2. **Conjoint Experiment:** Testing voter priorities (ideology vs. specific issues).
3. **Regression Discontinuity (RDiT):** Measuring the long-term causal effect of the 1988 plebiscite.

## Useful claims

- Ideological identification in Chile acts as a "social identity" that substitutes for weak political parties.
- Voters care more about a candidate's ideological label than their specific stance on issues (like immigration).
- For topic modeling with short texts, using a very low "k" (k=2) is better to avoid overlapping topics and ensure interpretability.
- High-intensity historical events (the 1988 plebiscite) can lock in ideological identities for decades.
