## What this source is about

This study compares how people use seven different social media platforms for politics in the US, UK, France, and Canada. It finds a "funnel" effect: many people use the apps, but only a few post about politics. Most importantly, it shows that right-wing users are much more likely to post political content than left-wing users, especially on private apps like WhatsApp.

## Method or approach

The researchers used a large online survey of over 12,000 people across four countries and used statistical models to analyze the results.

## Case, country, platform, and period

Country / region: US, UK, France, and Canada.
Election or political context: 2019 (Canada/UK) and 2020 (US) elections.
Platform: Facebook, Instagram, Twitter (X), YouTube, Reddit, Snapchat, and WhatsApp.
Time period: 2019–2021.
Type of material studied: Survey data from 12,302 respondents.

## Useful claims

- Digital behavior follows a "funnel": many adopt the platform, fewer see politics, and very few actually post.
- Right-wing users are more active in posting political content across all seven platforms.
- "Closed" apps like WhatsApp and Snapchat create "safe spaces" or echo chambers for polarized political sharing.
- Facebook is the "exception" because it has a mix of different views and people often avoid politics there.
- Platforms are not neutral; their specific design (affordances) actively shapes how people behave.
