### 1. Bibliographic Identity
*   **Bibliographic Identity:** Ituassu, A. (2023). Postmodern Without Modernization: Ages, Phases, and Stages of Political Communication and Digital Campaigns in Brazil (2010–2020). *International Journal of Communication*, 17, 3133–3153.
*   **What the source studies:** The historical evolution of political communication and digital campaigning in Brazil over a decade (2010–2020). Specifically, the intersection of digital media access, campaign regulation, and political radicalization.
*   **Method:** Historical perspective and contextual description. Uses canonical historical frameworks (Blumler, Norris, Swanson & Mancini) to map "ages, phases, and stages" of political communication.
*   **Corpus:** Brazilian electoral laws (HGPE, 1998–2017), media system statistics (access rates, Globo dominance), and specific political actors/elections (Bolsonaro 2018, Lula 2022, Collor 1989).
*   **Core Claims:**
    1.  Brazil exhibits a "postmodern without modernization" condition (universal media access without political autonomy).
    2.  Digital campaigns in Brazil led to "radicalization" (aggression against institutions) rather than "normalization."
    3.  "Equalization" occurred (minorities/outsiders like Bolsonaro succeeded), disproving the idea that digital media always favors majorities.
*   **Relevance to Diego's paper:** Provides a necessary regional comparative baseline (Brazil vs. Chile). It highlights that "digitalization" does not follow a universal Western path, supporting Diego's need for a distinct LatAm theoretical case.
*   **Cluster Tags:** #HistoricalAnalysis #BrazilPolitics #DigitalCampaigns #Radicalization #Platformization #LatAmMedia.
*   **Possible Citation Uses:** "As Ituassu (2023) demonstrates, digital access in Latin America can lead to political radicalization rather than modernization..." or "This study offers a historicized framework... unlike the Chilean context..."
*   **Limits:** Focuses exclusively on Brazil; does not cover the 2019 Chile uprising or the 2021 Chilean constitutional process.
*   **Open Questions:** How does the "captured liberal model" of Brazil compare to Chile's party fragmentation? Does the "equalization" effect seen in Bolsonaro apply to Chilean candidates?

### 3. Concept of Digitalization/Platformization
*   **Concept:** "Postmodern without modernization." Digitalization is framed as a "Hybrid media system" where universal access coexists with persistent social gaps.
*   **Actors:** Professional technocrats, traditional party elites, and "grassroots-based citizen-initiated campaigning" (e.g., Bolsonaro's movement).
*   **Platform Framing:** Treated as **active infrastructure** (changing political logic) rather than passive channels. Mentions "controlled interactivity" and "hybrid mass-target communication."
*   **Geographic Scope:** Brazil (with references to US/UK as ideal types for comparison).
*   **Mechanisms Linking Platforms to Strategy:** "Centrifugal diversification," "Segmentation," and "Data Science." Platforms allow directing public opinion (Bolsonaro) rather than obeying it.
*   **Chile 2021 Bridge Sentence:** *Synthesized for Diego's paper:* "In contrast to the Brazilian trajectory of radicalization through digital platforms, the 2021 Chilean constitutional process illustrates a different mechanism of digital mobilization aimed at systemic reform."
