### 2. Theoretical Object & Mechanism Extraction

*   **Theoretical Object:**
    *   **Digital Machine:** The "digital mode of production" and the tools within it.
    *   **Fixed Capital:** Traditionally physical machines; redefined here to include "cognitive matter" and "general intellect."
    *   **Living Labor:** The worker as both substance and active engine of production.
*   **Power Mechanism:**
    *   **Traditional:** Capital commands the machine; worker is subjected.
    *   **Digital:** Living labor merges with the machine, potentially reversing the relationship. Capital appropriates the "general intellect," but living labor also appropriates the machine.
*   **Platform Mediation (Channel vs. Infrastructure):**
    *   **Status:** The text treats the "digital machine" as an **active infrastructure** (a "machine" and "competitive antagonist"), not a passive channel.
    *   **Theorization:** It is conceptualized as a hybrid human/machine configuration where the worker *invests* in fixed capital.
*   **Portable Concepts:**
    *   **Appropriation of Fixed Capital:** The idea that workers take ownership/control over the means of production (cognitive tools).
    *   **General Intellect:** The collective intellectual power incorporated into the production process.
    *   **Ontological Autonomy:** The idea that work defines the worker's being/essence, even under control.
*   **What to Exclude:**
    *   Empirical studies of specific algorithms or political advertising strategies.
    *   Specifics of "digital labor" as gig-economy work (focus is on the cognitive mode, not the platform's intermediary role).
*   **3 Conceptual Building Blocks:**
    1.  **Cognitive Labor:** The worker contributes mental/intellectual value directly to the machine.
    2.  **Hybrid Configuration:** The merging of the human (living labor) and the tool (fixed capital).
    3.  **Reversal of Dominance:** The shift where living labor becomes a hegemonic subject capable of dispossessing capital.
