## Paragraph 1: Operationalizing Granularity (Resolving the "Micro" Debate)
**Paragraph Title: Operationalizing Granularity: Metadata Audits as the Empirical Core**

_Use these embeddings to transition from the theoretical debate on granularity to the methodological solution. Explain how auditing Facebook metadata (age, region, gender) provides the empirical capacity to test whether campaigns are truly engaging in hyper-niche targeting or disguised broadcasting, addressing Baldwin-Philippi's call for empirical grounding._

> To move beyond the speculative deadlocks of digital political communication, the theoretical debate on granularity must be resolved through direct empirical verification. While early definitions of microtargeting emphasize extreme niche-ism and psychographic profiles (CF01_Microtargeting_Definitions), empirical scholars have questioned the practical viability of these practices, arguing that budget constraints and platform limitations push campaigns toward more pragmatic uses of technology (Kreiss, 2016). Baldwin-Philippi (2018) argues that studies must ground their arguments in actual campaign practices rather than relying on the self-serving rhetoric of technology vendors. The methodological solution proposed in this research to address this call is a systematic metadata audit (CF_Methodology_Metadata_Audits). Auditing Facebook’s target audience metadata (including age, region, and gender criteria) provides the empirical capacity to measure the exact level of demographic segmentation candidates used during the 2021 Chilean election. This method is uniquely capable of testing whether candidates truly targeted narrow niches or whether they deployed a strategy of disguised broadcasting to reach broad groups of voters, thereby resolving the debate on granularity with material evidence.

## Paragraph 2: Computational Contextualization (Tracing Ideological Coherence)
**Paragraph Title: Computational Contextualization: Latent Dirichlet Allocation and Ideological Coherence**

_Use these embeddings to justify the use of Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA). Explain that because the Chilean context is characterized by institutional fragmentation and new political actors, LDA provides the capacity to uncover latent thematic structures and test if digital campaigns amplify pre-existing ideological divisions or create new ones._

> Analyzing campaign strategies in the wake of Chile's party system collapse requires a method capable of mapping complex thematic shifts across a fragmented ideological field (NB_Chile_Ideological_Fragmentation). Traditional content analysis falls short when faced with thousands of distinct digital ads produced in candidate-centered campaigns where political communication is highly personalized (CF04_Personalization_Digital_Campaigns). As Metz (2019) argues, digital personalization centers the candidate as the primary ideological vehicle, leading to highly customized thematic outputs. To systematically parse this personalized communication, this research utilizes Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) topic modeling (CF_Methodology_LDA_Topic_Modeling). As an unsupervised text-as-data method, LDA provides the capacity to discover latent thematic structures across candidate ad libraries without pre-defining categories. This approach is uniquely suited to test whether digital campaign communications amplified pre-existing ideological divisions within the Chilean electorate or whether they constructed new, fragmented issues to mobilize supporters during the post-uprising crisis.

## Paragraph 3: Assessing Manipulation (Testing Message Congruence)
**Paragraph Title: Assessing Manipulation: Message Congruence and Targeted Emphasis**

_Use these embeddings to address the ethics debate. Explain how integrating LDA topics with demographic metadata allows the research to test for "message congruence," providing the empirical capacity to distinguish between a logic of "targeted emphasis" and a logic of "fundamental manipulation" (contradictory messaging)._

> The normative debate surrounding the ethics of microtargeting centers on the potential for campaigns to engage in voter manipulation and democratic deception. Under the critical perspective, microtargeting is theorized as an opaque instrument of behavioral manipulation that enables candidates to send contradictory promises to different groups, leading to a fragmented and deceptive public sphere (Gorton, 2016). Conversely, more optimistic accounts suggest that targeting acts as a tool for targeted emphasis, aligning campaign messages with pre-existing voter issue interests without necessarily resorting to deceit (Kreiss, 2017). To resolve this fundamental debate (CF02_Democratic_Impact_Debates), this research proposes a methodology of message congruence testing (CF_Methodology_Congruence_Testing). By cross-tabulating the latent thematic topics identified through LDA with the targeting metadata, this method provides the capacity to evaluate the consistency of candidate messaging across different targeted demographic audiences. Specifically, it allows the research to determine whether candidate messaging was contradictory across age or regional boundaries, or whether campaigns maintained a stable core message while shifting emphasis, thereby providing an empirical test to distinguish between a logic of targeted emphasis and a logic of fundamental manipulation.
