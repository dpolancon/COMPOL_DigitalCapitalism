## Paragraph 1: The North American Baseline
**Paragraph Title: The North American Baseline: Psychographics and the Dystopian Lens**

_Use these embeddings to establish the dominant Global North definition of microtargeting as extreme niche-ism and psychographic manipulation, setting up the baseline terminology and the "dystopian" fears that the rest of the thesis will interrogate._

> The study of digital political communication in the Global North has largely coalesced around a definition of microtargeting centered on psychographic personalization and extreme audience fragmentation. As conceptualized by Borgesius et al. (2018) and López Ortega (2021), digital political advertising leverages granular voter profiles to deliver highly targeted messages tailored to individual psychological vulnerabilities. This conceptualization has fueled a dystopian anxiety within democratic theory, positioning microtargeting as an omnipotent mechanism of behavioral manipulation that threatens to dissolve the shared public sphere. However, this preoccupation with the persuasive content of individual ad copies creates a methodological blind spot. To evaluate whether these concerns hold empirical weight, it is necessary to transition from studying ad text in isolation to auditing the structural distribution of ad metadata. By applying a systematic analysis of Facebook's target audience metadata (CF_Methodology_Copy_vs_Metadata), researchers can map the actual granularity of campaign reach. This method provides the capacity to test whether campaigns are truly targeting narrow demographic niches or simply employing digital platforms for disguised broadcasting, thereby grounding theoretical fears in material evidence.

## Paragraph 2: The Epistemic Gap (The "Black Box" Problem)
**Paragraph Title: The Epistemic Gap: Platform Infrastructure and the Black Box**

_Use Kreiss to pivot from the definition of microtargeting to its practical limitations. Use CF02 to argue that by focusing only on ad copy (the baseline established in Para 1), the literature misses the actual infrastructure. This creates the epistemic gap that justifies our research._

> Despite the dominance of the dystopian manipulation model, empirical researchers argue that microtargeting is heavily constrained by organizational budgets, platform algorithms, and political realities, functioning more as a tool for targeted emphasis than deception (Kreiss, 2017). Yet, a significant epistemic gap remains: by focusing almost exclusively on ad copy, political communication literature treats platforms as neutral channels and leaves the actual mechanism of targeting as a "black box" (CF02_BlackBox_Platformization). This oversight ignores how platforms operate as active political actors that shape visibility and extract data for capital accumulation. To bridge this gap, this research proposes a methodological shift from message-centric analyses to metadata audits (CF_Methodology_Metadata_Shift). By analyzing the backend targeting metadata—including age, region, and gender segmentation—this approach possesses the capacity to expose the platformized campaign infrastructure. Rather than merely documenting the rhetoric candidates project, this method enables the systematic evaluation of how platform structures and candidate strategies interact to distribute visibility, thereby making the platform's proprietary targeting mechanism legible to public scrutiny.

## Paragraph 3: The Transferability Problem (Moving to Chile)
**Paragraph Title: The Transferability Problem: Situating Digital Politics in Chile**

_Use Tejada to show that Chilean literature exists but is disconnected from microtargeting. Use CF05 to argue that applying the Global North baseline to Chile ignores the specific constitutive context of the 2021 post-uprising institutional crisis. The literature assumes stable institutions; our case does not have them._

> Applying campaign models developed in the Global North directly to the Latin American context introduces a serious transferability problem. In Chile, while scholarly work on digital political communication exists, it has historically focused on general social network usage and remained disconnected from the specific structural dynamics of online microtargeting (Tejada, 2016). This disconnection is particularly problematic given that the 2021 Chilean elections occurred in a highly volatile, post-uprising (*estallido social*) context of deep institutional crisis and constitutional rewrite (CF05_Constitutive_Context_LatAm). Unlike the stable party systems assumed in North American literature, the Chilean case represents a moment of structural dislocation where traditional coalitions fractured and anti-establishment campaigns emerged. To resolve this transferability gap, we employ Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) topic modeling (CF_Methodology_LDA_Contextualization). By applying LDA, this research is uniquely equipped to discover the latent thematic topics within candidate ads and trace how they map onto different demographic targeting segments. This computational approach allows us to analyze how digital campaign strategies are situated within Chile’s crisis context, offering a situated theory of platform-mediated political communication in the Global South rather than importing foreign conceptual frameworks.
