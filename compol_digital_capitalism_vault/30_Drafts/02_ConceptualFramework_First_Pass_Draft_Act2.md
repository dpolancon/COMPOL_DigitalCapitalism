## Paragraph 1: The Legitimacy Crisis and Bloc Dislocation
**Paragraph Title: The Legitimacy Crisis and Bloc Dislocation: The Digital Imperative in Fractured Systems**

_Use these embeddings to explain how the 2019 social uprising and the subsequent collapse of the post-dictatorship party system created a vacuum of representation, making traditional mass media strategies insufficient and necessitating direct digital outreach to a distrustful electorate._

> The 2021 Chilean presidential campaign occurred within a unique, highly volatile socio-political conjuncture that served as a constitutive context for campaign strategy. Following the 2019 social uprising (*estallido social*), the country experienced a deep institutional legitimacy crisis, characterized by a rapid decline of the traditional post-dictatorship party system (NB_Chile_Party_System_Decline). As Argote et al. (2025) note, while ideological stability persisted among voters, the institutional structures of representation deteriorated, creating a profound representation vacuum. In this context of institutional dislocation, traditional mass media strategies became structurally insufficient for reaching a highly distrustful and fragmented electorate. Consequently, campaigns turned to social media platforms to establish unmediated, direct communication channels, using these interfaces to bypass discredited traditional party machines and legacy media gatekeepers (Luna et al., 2022). To understand how campaigns navigated this vacuum, this study employs a metadata demographic analysis (CF_Methodology_Metadata_Demographics). This methodological approach is uniquely capable of auditing how campaigns structured their target audiences by age, region, and gender, revealing whether candidate strategies targeted specific demographic sub-segments to rebuild voter coalitions or maintained broad-based outreach within a fractured political landscape.

## Paragraph 2: The Material Catalyst: Pandemic Restrictions and Digital Acceleration
**Paragraph Title: The Material Catalyst: Pandemic Constraints and Platformized Politics**

_Use Fuchs and the context notes to argue that the physical impossibility of in-person campaigning due to the pandemic intersected with the political crisis, forcing campaigns to subordinate their communication entirely to the algorithmic logic and infrastructure of platform capitalism._

> While the political crisis generated the structural need for direct digital outreach, the COVID-19 pandemic in 2021 served as the material catalyst that accelerated the platformization of political campaigning (NB_Chile_2021_Election_Context). The physical impossibility of traditional in-person campaigning forced political actors to transfer their communication strategies almost entirely to social media infrastructures. This shift did not represent a neutral relocation of public debate, but rather the subordination of democratic communication to the logic of platform capitalism (CF03_Platformization_Of_Politics). As Fuchs (2019) argues, digital platforms are profit-driven enterprises that monetize user communication through the extraction of attention and data. By relying on Facebook's advertising system, campaigns became structurally dependent on corporate algorithms and proprietary ad delivery systems (Fuchs, 2019). To capture the dynamics of this adaptation, this research implements a temporal volume analysis (CF_Methodology_Temporal_Volume_Analysis). This analytical method provides the capacity to track the volume and distribution of ad publications over time. It allows researchers to investigate how campaigns adapted their intensity to critical political turning points, mapping the precise temporal pacing through which democratic communication was subordinated to platformized infrastructures.

## Paragraph 3: "New Politics" and the Rise of Platform-Centric Candidacies
**Paragraph Title: "New Politics" and the Rise of Platform-Centric Candidacies**

_Use Gerbaudo to frame the emergence of candidates from the semi-periphery (like Parisi, Boric, Kast) not just as political anomalies, but as the embodiment of the "platform party," where digital interfaces replace traditional mass media gatekeepers and party machines._

> The erosion of traditional party hierarchies paved the way for highly personalized, candidate-centric campaigns that bypassed established party machines. The emergence of candidates from the political semi-periphery—such as Gabriel Boric on the left, José Antonio Kast on the far-right, and Franco Parisi campaigning entirely via digital networks from the United States—represents the materialization of the "platform party" model (Gerbaudo, 2019). Under this model, digital interfaces and personalized media networks replace traditional bureaucratic gatekeepers, enabling candidates to assemble direct networks of supporters around their personal brands (CF04_Personalization_Digital_Campaigns). As Álvarez-Fuentes et al. (2024) demonstrate, campaigns utilized participatory formats, such as political memes, to generate digital engagement and foster a sense of collective identity among fragmented groups of voters. To dissect how these personalized campaigns built their bases, this study utilizes an analysis of targeting segmentation strategies (CF_Methodology_Segmentation_Strategies). This methodology is uniquely equipped to identify the configuration of targeting criteria used by each candidate, evaluating how campaigns structured their outreach to distinct demographic and regional groups, thereby clarifying the mechanisms through which platform-mediated personalization replaces traditional party organizations.
