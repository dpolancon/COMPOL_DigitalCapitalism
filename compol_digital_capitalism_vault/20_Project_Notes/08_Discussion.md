## 8. Discussion: The Political Economy of Microtargeting in a Fragmented Democracy

The empirical analysis of the 2021 Chilean presidential election Facebook Ad Library reveals a complex reality that challenges both the apocalyptic narratives of digital manipulation and the optimistic assumptions of participatory digital democracy. By operationalizing microtargeting through a formal structural typology—utilizing the Relative Exposure Index (REI), Topic Targeting Divergence (TTD), and Targeting Gini Coefficient (TGC)—this study moves beyond speculative fears to document the actual mechanics of data-driven campaigning in a Global South context. The findings suggest that Chilean microtargeting in 2021 was not a tool of surgical, psychographic manipulation, but rather a pragmatic, structurally constrained strategy of _thematic emphasis_, deeply embedded in the political economy of platform capitalism and the specific institutional dislocations of post-2019 Chile.

### 8.1. The Reality of Microtargeting: Pragmatic Broadcasting vs. Surgical Precision

The international literature frequently characterizes microtargeting as the delivery of hyper-personalized, narrowly tailored messages to psychographic niches (Borgesius et al., 2018; López Ortega, 2021). However, our empirical results mandate a significant downward revision of this "surgical precision" claim.

The TTD and TGC indices reveal a mixed strategic reality. While real demographic segmentation existed (e.g., specific age tranches and regions receiving distinct thematic content), the vast majority of ads exhibited low TTD and low TGC scores, indicating a _broadcast_ or _strategically differentiated_ approach rather than surgical targeting. Many ads carried multiple, low-weight targeting decisions (e.g., <1% weight on specific regions), suggesting that campaigns were often conducting A/B audience testing rather than executing definitive micro-delivery.

This finding strongly supports Baldwin-Philippi’s (2018) assertion that real-world data-driven campaigning is driven by pragmatism, not ideology. The power of microtargeting in the Chilean context lies more in its _potential capacity_ and the infrastructural affordances it provides, rather than in the exercised practice of extreme niche-ism, which is constrained by campaign budgets, platform pricing structures, and the limited granularity of the public Ad Library metadata.

### 8.2. The Political Economy of the 2021 Crisis: Microtargeting as a Rational Response to Institutional Dislocation

To understand _why_ campaigns adopted these specific digital strategies, we must situate the 2021 election within Chile’s profound institutional dislocation. The collapse of the traditional center-left (Concertación) and center-right (Alianza) coalitions following the 2019 _estallido social_ created a legitimacy crisis. Outsider candidates (Boric, Kast, Parisi) lacked the consolidated mass-media presence and traditional party machinery of their predecessors.

Viewed through a **Digital Capitalism** lens (Fuchs & Chandler, 2019), the Facebook Ad Library is not merely a transparency tool; it is the interface of _platform rent extraction_. In a regulatory vacuum governed by the outdated Ley 19.628 (1999), campaigns were forced to rent Meta’s algorithmic infrastructure to reach a distrustful, fragmented electorate. Microtargeting was not an optional technological upgrade; it was a _rational, structural necessity_ for political survival. Candidates like Franco Parisi, who ran a 100% digital campaign from abroad, exemplify the extreme of this dynamic, treating the electorate not as a public to be deliberated with, but as a dataset to be efficiently harvested and mobilized via platform affordances.

### 8.3. Asymmetric Coherence: Ideological Fragmentation and Thematic Emphasis

The LDA topic modeling, coupled with the REI panels, reveals a stark asymmetry in how political sectors utilized the platform.

- **The Left (Boric):** Exhibited high thematic coherence. The REI matrices show a consistent, guiding axis of social rights (health, education, pensions, care) targeted broadly but with specific regional emphases (e.g., emphasizing public services in marginalized zones).
- **The Right (Kast, Sichel):** Exhibited lower thematic coherence and higher fragmentation. The TTD rankings show a wider dispersion of topics, reflecting the greater ideological distance between the neoliberal-centrist wing (Sichel) and the far-right populist wing (Kast).

Crucially, the cross-round cosine similarity analysis demonstrates that microtargeting _amplifies_ pre-existing ideological differences; it does not create them. As campaigns moved from the first to the second round, we observed formal "topic births" and "topic deaths" (e.g., Kast’s shift toward gender and immigration themes; Boric’s pivot toward security and institutional order). This confirms that digital segmentation is used to emphasize existing partisan cleavages to mobilize the base, rather than to dynamically construct new, contradictory identities for different voters.

### 8.4. Separating Rhetoric from Reality: Emphasis, Not Manipulation

A central debate in the literature pits dystopian fears of manipulative microtargeting (Gorton, 2016) against more grounded assessments of democratic risk (Kreiss, 2017). Our findings decisively support the latter.

By cross-referencing topic assignments with demographic REI scores, we found **no evidence of candidates sending fundamentally contradictory messages** to different demographic groups to deceive them. Boric remained consistently pro-reform across all segments; Kast remained consistently pro-security and pro-family. The variation observed was strictly one of _thematic emphasis_ (e.g., highlighting security more heavily in the Araucanía region), not factual contradiction.

This validates Kreiss’s (2017) thesis: fears about microtargeting often reflect pre-existing, broader anxieties about democratic decay and polarization, rather than empirical evidence of novel, algorithmically-driven manipulation. The Chilean case demonstrates that campaigns operate in a logic of _targeted emphasis_, matching existing messages to receptive audiences, rather than manufacturing bespoke falsehoods for isolated niches.

### 8.5. Implications for Digital Capitalism and Democratic Regulation

This study contributes to the political economy of communication by demonstrating how platform capitalism reshapes electoral competition in the Global South. The findings carry three major implications:

1. **The Limits of "Transparency":** The Facebook Ad Library, while a vital empirical resource, documents only a strict subset of the targeting parameters available to advertisers. It captures the _outcome_ of algorithmic sorting, but obscures the "correlational machine" (Chandler, 2019) and the extraction of digital labor that fuels it. True transparency requires regulatory mandates that go beyond Meta’s voluntary, self-policing archives.
2. **The Shift from Persuasion to Mobilization:** Consistent with Anstead et al. (2025), the platform’s pricing architecture incentivizes campaigns to mobilize existing supporters via polarizing wedge issues rather than persuade undecided median voters. Microtargeting thus structurally reinforces affective polarization, not through hidden manipulation, but through the rational economic logic of platform advertising.
3. **The Invisible Channels:** The visible, regulated space of Facebook paid ads represents only one layer of the digital campaign. Future research must urgently address the "dark spaces" of political communication—WhatsApp, SMS, and organic algorithmic amplification (e.g., TikTok OACPs)—where the most unregulated and potentially manipulative forms of microtargeting likely occur.

In conclusion, the 2021 Chilean election demonstrates that data-driven campaigning is not a monolithic, dystopian force, but a highly constrained, pragmatic tool shaped by local institutional crises and the global logic of platform rent extraction. Understanding this nuance is essential for developing regulatory frameworks that address the real risks of digital capitalism without succumbing to informational moral panics.
